## 2. 《Shape Up》章节级要点（英文原句 + 中文要点）

**书目事实**：书名 *Shape Up: Stop Running in Circles and Ship Work that Matters*，作者 Ryan Singer（独著），Jason Fried 作序。
写作年份 **[一手]**：Singer 本人在 ryansinger.co 写明 "I wrote Shape Up in 2019 to formalize how we did product development over my 17 years at 37signals."（[P22](https://www.ryansinger.co)）
结构：Part 1 Shaping（Ch.2–6）／Part 2 Betting（Ch.7–9）／Part 3 Building（Ch.10–15）／Appendices（6 篇）。
官方免费全文：https://basecamp.com/shapeup —— **[一手]**，与纸质版同源。

> **注意一个易错点**：全书**没有**名为「Six-week cycles / Why six weeks / Cool-down / Small teams / Expectations 与 QA / 不能中途加人」的独立章节标题。这些概念**分散在各章的二级小节里**（见下表 URL 锚点）。若后续蒸馏时把「Why six weeks」当作章节标题引用，会编造出书里不存在的结构。

### 2.0 Jason Fried 序言（Foreword）

URL: https://basecamp.com/shapeup/0.1-foreword ｜ **[一手]**

> "The way a team works has an enormous influence on what it can do."
> "You'll often hear people say 'execution is everything,' but that's not quite right. In fact, it's often quite wrong."
> "Sometimes execution is everything—everything that's wrong."
> "For one, we're not into waterfall or agile or scrum. For two, we don't line walls with Post-it notes. For three, we don't do daily stand ups, design sprints, development sprints, or anything remotely tied to a metaphor that includes being tired and worn out at the end. No backlogs, no Kanban, no velocity tracking, none of that."
> "Don't think of this as a book. Think of it as a flashlight."

**中文要点**：Fried 把「执行力就是一切」当作误命题——错误的执行方式会摧毁士气、磨掉信任、碾碎长期动能。37signals **明确不在** waterfall / agile / scrum 谱系内，且不设 backlog、Kanban、velocity tracking、daily standup、sprint。全书定位是「手电筒」而非教科书。

**⚠️ 这是后面第 8 节的核心矛盾之一**：一手文本明确拒绝 agile/scrum，但市面上大量二手总结把 Shape Up 归入「敏捷方法论之一」。

### 2.1 Ch.1 Introduction

URL: https://basecamp.com/shapeup/0.3-chapter-01 ｜ **[一手]**

> "First, we work in **six-week cycles**. Six weeks is long enough to build something meaningful start-to-finish and short enough that everyone can feel the deadline looming from the start, so they use the time wisely."
> "Second, we **shape the work** before giving it to a team."
> "Instead of asking how much time it will _take_ to do some work, we ask: How much time do we want to _spend_? How much is this idea worth?"
> "Third, we **give full responsibility** to a small integrated team of designers and programmers. They define their own tasks, make adjustments to the scope, and work together to build vertical slices of the product one at a time. This is completely different from other methodologies, where managers chop up the work and programmers act like ticket-takers."
> "Together, these concepts form a virtuous circle. When teams are more autonomous, senior people can spend less time managing them. With less time spent on management, senior people can shape up better projects. When projects are better shaped, teams have clearer boundaries and so can work more autonomously."
> "At every step of the process we target a specific risk: the risk of not shipping on time. This book isn't about the risk of building the wrong thing."
> "You can have the best strategy in the world, but if you can't act on it, what good does it do?"
> "We reduce risk in the planning process by capping our bets to six weeks. If a project runs over, by default it doesn't get an extension. This 'circuit breaker' ensures that we don't invest multiples of the original appetite on a concept that needs rethinking first."
> "David only worked ten hours a week."（2003 年 Basecamp 第一版，DHH 每周只投入 10 小时）
> "Our intense focus on 'hammering' the scope to fit within a given time budget was born under these constraints."

**中文要点**：
1. **六周周期**——长到能做完有意义的事，短到从第一天就感到截止日压着。
2. **先 shaping 再接活**——资深小组与周期团队并行工作，把方案的关键元素定下来；抽象层级要「足够具体让团队知道做什么，又足够抽象留出发挥空间」。
3. **整队负责**——小型设计+开发混合团队，自己定任务、自己调 scope，逐个交付垂直切片；不是经理切任务、程序员当「取票员」。
4. **三者构成正向循环**：团队越自主 → 资深者越少花时间管理 → shaping 质量越高 → 边界越清晰 → 团队越自主。
5. **本书瞄准的风险是「没按时发布」，不是「做错东西」**。Singer 直言：发现流程（discovery）应该排在「恢复发布能力」之后。他推荐 *Competing Against Luck* 处理「做对东西」的问题。
6. **Origin story**：2003 年 Basecamp v1 由三人做出，DHH 每周只工作 10 小时——「hammering the scope」的做法诞生于这个约束。2009 年做产品套件整合时首次试验 breadboarding 与 scope mapping；2012 年 Basecamp 2.0 重做时复用；2015 年团队扩到四倍且全员远程，急需语言来描述做法，遂转向固定周期，并正式化 pitch / betting 流程。Singer 的角色从设计+PM 转到产品策略，因此需要「shaping」这个词。

### 2.2 Ch.2 Principles of Shaping

URL: https://basecamp.com/shapeup/1.1-chapter-02 ｜ **[一手]**

> "When we shape the work, we need to do it at the right level of abstraction: not too vague and not too concrete."
> "Over-specifying the design also leads to estimation errors. Counterintuitive as it may seem, the more specific the work is, the harder it can be to estimate."
> "When a project is defined in a few words, nobody knows what it means."
> "You're solving a problem with no context. You have to be a mind reader. It's like: 'we'll know it when we see it.'"（引用一位程序员的抱怨）
> **Property 1: It's rough** — "Work that's too fine, too early commits everyone to the wrong details."
> **Property 2: It's solved** — "Despite being rough and unfinished, shaped work has been thought through. All the main elements of the solution are there at the macro level and they connect together."
> **Property 3: It's bounded** — "Lastly, shaped work indicates what _not_ to do. It tells the team where to stop."
> "Taken together, the roughness leaves room for the team to resolve all the details, while the solution and boundaries act like guard rails."

**中文要点**：
- **Wireframes 太具体**：过早给高保真稿会剥夺设计师的创作空间，而且**越具体越难估时**（因为「界面刚刚好」可能牵出看不见的实现复杂度）；scope 不可变时团队无法重新权衡。
- **Words 太抽象**：「做个日历视图」这种一句话需求，团队没有交易（trade-off）依据，也不知道该包含/排除什么，必然失控。
- **shaping 三性质**：rough（粗糙，看得出未完成，留出贡献空间）／solved（宏观层面已想通、元素互相咬合）／bounded（明确说什么**不**做）。
- **谁来 shaping**：shaping 是创意+整合工作，主要属于设计工作（交互设计，从用户视角定义「做什么、怎么运作、落在现有流程哪里」），但**必须技术通识**——不必会写代码，但要能判断可行性、难易。同时是策略工作：设 appetite、批判问题、判断谁受影响、机会成本。
- **双轨制（Two tracks）**：shaping 无法排期（未 shaped 的工作本质上风险未知），因此分「shaping 轨」与「building 轨」，同一时间并行。shaping 轨的工作**保持私密**，直到决定下注才公开——好处是可以在不成功时把半成品搁置或丢弃。
- **Case study: Dot Grid Calendar**——旧版 Basecamp 只有约 10% 客户用日历功能，因此对「花六个月做日历」没有 appetite；退化成「两月只读网格 + 事件打点 + 下方列表」，明确放弃拖拽、跨天事件、颜色分类。
- **shaping 四步**：① Set boundaries ② Rough out the elements ③ Address risks and rabbit holes ④ Write the pitch。

### 2.3 Ch.3 Set Boundaries

URL: https://basecamp.com/shapeup/1.2-chapter-03 ｜ **[一手]**

> "We call this the `appetite`. You can think of the appetite as a time budget for a standard team size."
> "`Small Batch`: This is a project that a team of one designer and one or two programmers can build in one or two weeks."
> "`Big Batch`: This project takes the same-size team a full six-weeks."
> **"An appetite is completely different from an estimate. Estimates start with a design and end with a number. Appetites start with a number and end with a design. We use the appetite as a creative constraint on the design process."**
> "This principle, called 'fixed time, variable scope,' is key to successfully defining and shipping projects."
> "With one week left, I can choose between fixing typos or adding a new section to a chapter. That's the tension between time, quality, and scope. ... Without the pressure of the fixed deadline, I wouldn't make the trade-off."
> "There's no absolute definition of 'the best' solution. The best is relative to your constraints. ... The ultimate meal might be a ten course dinner. But when you're hungry and in a hurry, a hot dog is perfect."
> "Our default response to any idea that comes up should be: 'Interesting. Maybe some day.' In other words, a very soft 'no' that leaves all our options open. We don't put it in a backlog."
> "When it comes to unclear ideas, the worst offenders are 'redesigns' or 'refactorings' that aren't driven by a single problem or use case. When someone proposes something like 'redesign the Files section,' that's a grab-bag, not a project."
> "A tell-tale sign of a grab-bag is the '2.0' label."

**中文要点**：
- **Appetite ≠ Estimate**——这是全书最常被引用的定义，原文对仗极工整：**estimate 从设计出发到数字结束；appetite 从数字出发到设计结束**。appetite 是设计过程的创造性约束。
- **两档 appetite**：Small Batch（1 设计 + 1~2 程序，1–2 周）／Big Batch（同规模团队 6 周）。若 6 周都装不下，先 hammer 问题定义；还不行就切出能装进 6 周的那部分。
- **Fixed time, variable scope**——Singer 用自己写这本书举例：只剩一周时，必须在「修错别字」与「加新章节」之间选；他选修错别字（降 scope 保质量）。若 scope 不可变，就**必须**加新章节，然后没有时间修质量。
- **"Good" is relative**——没有绝对最优解，最优相对于约束。饿了赶时间时，热狗就是完美的。
- **对 raw idea 的默认回应是柔和的「不」**：「Interesting. Maybe some day.」保持所有选项开放，不进 backlog。
- **Narrow down the problem**——把「客户要更复杂的权限规则」拆到底，发现真问题是「有人归档文件却不知道会让其他人看不到」，于是改成在归档动作上加一条警告：**一天的改动取代六周的项目**。
- **Case study: Defining "calendar"**——不问「你为什么想要日历、想要长什么样」，而问「**你什么时候**想要日历——起念头那一刻你在干什么？」答案：她在家办公时客户来电话约会议，她得开车去办公室看黑板上的大日历，路上堵车且没有合适空位。洞察不是「把日历电子化」（那是显然的），而是「**看空档**」才是关键用例。
- **Grab-bag 警告**：不针对单一问题或用例的「重设计」「重构」是杂货袋，不是项目；「2.0」是典型征兆。Basecamp 当年启动「Files 2.0」就直接翻车（不知道「做完」长什么样），后来拆成「更好的文件预览」「自定义文件夹颜色」才成功。
- **三要素齐备**＝raw idea + appetite + 收窄后的问题定义。

### 2.4 Ch.4 Find the Elements

URL: https://basecamp.com/shapeup/1.3-chapter-04 ｜ **[一手]**

> "First, we need to have the right people—or nobody—in the room."
> "We borrow a concept from electrical engineering to help us design at the right level of abstraction. A breadboard is an electrical engineering prototype that has all the components and wiring of a real device but no industrial design."
> "There are three basic things we'll draw: 1. **Places**: These are things you can navigate to, like screens, dialogs, or menus that pop up. 2. **Affordances**: These are things the user can act on, like buttons and fields. We consider interface copy to be an affordance, too. 3. **Connection lines**: These show how the affordances take the user from place to place."
> "We'll use words for everything instead of pictures."
> "A fat marker sketch is a sketch made with such broad strokes that adding detail is difficult or impossible."
> "It may seem a little silly to call fat marker sketches a technique or a tool. The reason for calling them out is we too easily skip ahead to the wrong level of fidelity. Giving this rough early stage a name and using a specific tool for it helps us to segment our own creative process."
> "This isn't a spec. It's more like the boundaries and rules of a game. It could go in countless different ways once it's time to play."
> "Also keep in mind that, at this stage, we could walk away from the project. We haven't bet on it."

**中文要点**：
- **Breadboarding**（借用电子工程概念）：电学面包板有真机的全部元件与连线，但没有工业设计。UI 面包板同理——只定义 affordance 与它们的连接，不定义视觉样式。
- **三种画法**：Places（可导航到的屏幕/弹窗/菜单，画名并下划线）／Affordances（按钮、输入框，画在线下方；**界面文案也算 affordance**）／Connection lines（箭头）。
- **刻意用文字而非图片**——重点在「识别出哪些组件、它们怎么连接」，从而把一条用例跑一遍，判断动作序列是否服务于用例。
- **Fat marker sketch**（粗马克笔草图）：笔画粗到无法添加细节。最初用粗头 Sharpie 在纸上画，现在也在 iPad 上把笔径调大。适用于「二维排布本身就是核心问题」的想法，此时 breadboard 会错失重点。
- **给这个粗糙阶段命名本身就是技术**——因为人太容易跳到错误保真度；命名+专用工具能切割创作过程，防止在一个想法还没被足够勘测时就冲进去细化。
- **Room for designers**——你不想对设计师说「我知道我画成这样，但请忽略」。无论你怎么说，具体 mockup 都会**带偏后来者**（尤其当你职位更高时）。留白＝给后续阶段留创作空间。
- **No conveyor belt**——这一步还没有下注、没有承诺；只是把 raw idea 变得更能落地，日后好去争取资源。

### 2.5 Ch.5 Risks and Rabbit Holes

URL: https://basecamp.com/shapeup/1.4-chapter-05 ｜ **[一手]**

> "Suppose we bet on the project and a team takes it on. If they run into an unanticipated problem that takes two weeks to solve, they just burned a third of the budget!"
> "We once bet on a project to redesign the way we present projects with clients on Basecamp's home screen. We assumed the designer would figure it out; we didn't do the work in the shaping phase to validate that a viable approach existed. ... None of us were able to find a suitable design solution within the six weeks we budgeted. We ended up abandoning the project and rethinking it later."
> "In terms of risk, well-shaped work looks like a **thin-tailed probability distribution**."
> "However, if there are any rabbit holes in the shaping—technical unknowns, unsolved design problems, or misunderstood interdependencies—the project could take _multiple times_ the original appetite to complete. The right tail stretches out."
> "It's not responsible to give the team a tangled knot of interdependencies and then ask them to untangle it within a short fixed time window."
> "As shapers, we're thinking less about the ultimate design and more about basic quality and risk."
> **"Beware the simple question: 'Is this possible?' In software, everything is possible but nothing is free. ... Instead of asking 'is it possible to do X?' ask 'is X possible in 6-weeks?' That's a very different question."**
> "Try to keep the clay wet."

**中文要点**：
- **薄尾 vs 厚尾分布**——well-shaped 的工作像薄尾分布（可能多花一周，但不会更多）；一旦有 rabbit hole，项目可能花**原 appetite 的数倍**，右尾拖长。
- **Rabbit hole 的官方定义（Glossary）**："Part of a project that is too unknown, complex, or open-ended to bet on."
- **自查问题清单**：是否需要从未做过的技术工作？是否假设了各部件能拼在一起？是否假设存在一个我们自己想不出的设计解？是否有该提前定下的硬决策？
- **Case study: Patching a hole**——To-Do Groups 项目引入「分隔线」后，发现没考虑「已完成项怎么显示」。这个洞若不补，就等于把深层设计问题丢给团队在死线下解决。**shaping 阶段的对策是直接指定方案（dictate a solution）**：已完成项保持原样、不分组，只在末尾附加组名；有点乱，但换来「砍掉一大截风险尾巴」。Singer 的评语：这种取舍在周期内压力下很难做出；shaping 者关心的是基础质量与风险，不是终极设计。
- **Declare out of bounds**——明确划出**不支持**的用例。案例：群组通知项目只做「发消息时更快选人」，明确排除「指派待办」「聊天@人」等场景。
- **Cut back**——把「激动但非必要」的部分砍掉（To-Do Groups 的彩色分组标签被降级为 nice-to-have）。
- **Present to technical experts**——shaping 到此为止都是闭门活动；写 pitch 之前找技术专家过一遍。沟通定调要「友好密谋式」："Here's something I'm thinking about… but I'm not ready to show anybody yet… what do you think?" 要**保持黏土是湿的**（别写文档、别做 PPT，把人拉到白板前重画一遍）。
- **注意**：Singer 在 2025 年的自我修正中明确指出，「任何在 shaping 阶段未被**解决**的 rabbit hole 都是定时炸弹」——见第 8 节。

### 2.6 Ch.6 Write the Pitch

URL: https://basecamp.com/shapeup/1.5-chapter-06 ｜ **[一手]**

> "There are five ingredients that we always want to include in a pitch: 1. **Problem** ... 2. **Appetite** ... 3. **Solution** ... 4. **Rabbit holes** ... 5. **No-gos** — Anything specifically excluded from the concept"
> "It's critical to always present both a problem and a solution together. ... Diving straight into 'what to build'—the solution—is dangerous. You don't establish any basis for discussing whether this solution is good or bad without a problem."
> "The best problem definition consists of a single specific story that shows why the status quo doesn't work."
> "You can think of the appetite as another part of the problem definition."
> "Anybody can suggest expensive and complicated solutions. It takes work and design insight to get to a simple idea that fits in a small time box."
> "That's not ready to pitch or bet on. A problem without a solution is unshaped work. Giving it to a team means pushing research and exploration down to the wrong level, where the skillsets, time limit, and risk profile (thin vs. heavy tailed) are all misaligned."
> "We prefer asynchronous communication by default and escalate to real-time only when necessary. This gives everyone the maximum amount of time under their own control for doing real work."

**中文要点**：
- **Pitch 五配料**：Problem / Appetite / Solution / Rabbit holes / No-gos。官方 Glossary 定义："A document that presents a shaped project idea for consideration at the betting table."
- **问题与方案必须同时出现**——只给方案会失去「适配性」的判据；只给问题则是 unshaped work（把研究与探索推到了错误的层级，技能配置、时限、风险形状全部错配）。
- **最好的问题定义是「一个具体故事」**，展示现状为何不work——这提供 baseline 用于检验方案适配度。
- 也要能识别人群：如果问题只发生在已知留存率低的客户身上，六周做出精妙方案也不值得。
- **Help them see it**——pitch 里要比闭门 shaping 时**更具体一点**（读者没有共同语境），但**不能过度具体**（wireframe/高保真会框死设计师，并把讨论带偏到颜色、比例、布局）。手段：Embedded sketches（在真实截图上叠粗笔草图）、Annotated fat marker sketches（用不同颜色/编号标注）。
- **呈现方式**：默认异步——先把 pitch 贴到 stakeholders 能自行阅读的地方，让 betting table 会议短而高效；必要时才拉实时会议。

### 2.7 Ch.7 Bets, Not Backlogs

URL: https://basecamp.com/shapeup/2.1-chapter-07 ｜ **[一手]**

> "Backlogs are a big weight we don't need to carry. Dozens and eventually hundreds of tasks pile up that we all know we'll never have time for. The growing pile gives us a feeling like we're always behind even though we're not."
> "Backlogs are big time wasters too. The time spent constantly reviewing, grooming and organizing old ideas prevents everyone from moving forward on the timely projects that really matter right now."
> "Nothing else is on the table. There's no giant list of ideas to review. ... There are just a few well-shaped, risk-reduced options to review."
> "If we decide to bet on a pitch, it goes into the next cycle to build. If we don't, we let it go. There's nothing we need to track or hold on to."
> "We don't have to choose between a burdensome backlog and not remembering anything from the past. Everyone can still track pitches, bugs, requests, or things they want to do independently without a central backlog."
> "This way the conversation is always fresh. Anything brought back is brought back with a context, by a person, with a purpose."
> "It's easy to overvalue ideas. The truth is, ideas are cheap."

**中文要点**：
- **不要 backlog**：堆积的任务制造「永远落后」的错觉，且持续 review/groom 旧想法是巨大的时间浪费。
- **每个六周周期前开 betting table**，只看**过去六周新 shaped 的 pitch**，或有人**刻意复活并重新游说**的一两个旧 pitch。桌上没有别的东西。
- **决定下注**→进下一周期；**不下注**→放掉，不需要跟踪或保留。
- **Decentralized lists**——各部门各自维护自己的清单（Support 维护高频请求、Product 维护想 shape 的想法、Programmer 维护想修的 bug），**这些清单都不是 betting 流程的直接输入**。跨部门定期一对一让想法交叉授粉。
- **Important ideas come back**——「真正重要的想法会自己回来」。忘了的、只听到一次的，可能本来就不是问题。

### 2.8 Ch.8 The Betting Table

URL: https://basecamp.com/shapeup/2.2-chapter-08 ｜ **[一手]**

> "Some companies use two-week cycles (aka 'sprints'). We learned that two weeks is too short to get anything meaningful done. Worse than that, two-week cycles are extremely costly due to the planning overhead."
> "People need to feel the deadline looming in order to make trade-offs. If the deadline is too distant and abstract at the start, teams will naturally wander and use time inefficiently."
> "After years of experimentation we arrived at `six weeks`. Six weeks is long enough to finish something meaningful and still short enough to see the end from the beginning."
> "Therefore, after each six-week cycle, we schedule two weeks for `cool-down`. This is a period with no scheduled work where we can breathe, meet as needed, and consider what to do next."
> "Our project teams consist of either one designer and two programmers or one designer and one programmer. They're joined by a QA person who does integration testing later in the cycle."
> "Our betting table at Basecamp consists of the CEO (who in our case is the last word on product), CTO, a senior programmer, and a product strategist (myself)."
> "The highest people in the company are there. There's no 'step two' to validate the plan or get approval. And nobody else can jump in afterward to interfere or interrupt the scheduled work."
> "We talk about 'betting' instead of planning because it sets different expectations. First, bets have a payout. ... Second, bets are commitments. ... Third, a smart bet has a cap on the downside."
> "When you make a bet, you honor it."
> "When people ask for 'just a few hours' or 'just one day,' don't be fooled. Momentum and progress are second-order things, like growth or acceleration. You can't describe them with one point. You need an uninterrupted curve of points. ... **Losing the wrong hour can kill a day. Losing a day can kill a week.**"
> "But true crises are very rare."
> "There is nothing special about bugs that makes them automatically more important than everything else."
> "It is crucial to maximize our options in the future."

**中文要点**：
- **为什么六周**：两周一 sprint 太短，做不出有意义的东西，且规划开销（sprint planning）与「打断所有人动量重新集合」的机会成本极高。六周是「长到能做完、短到从开头就能看见结尾」。**这是书里 "Why six weeks" 的实际出处**（小节标题为 Six-week cycles）。
- **Cool-down**：每周期后两周无排期工作，可喘气、按需开会、考虑下一步；同时是 programmers/designers 自由时间（修 bug、探索新想法、试新技术）。**官方 Glossary**："A two-week break between cycles to do ad-hoc tasks, fix bugs, and hold a betting table."
- **团队规模**：1 设计 + 2 程序，或 1 设计 + 1 程序；周期后段加入 1 名 QA 做集成测试。**Big batch** 团队整周期做一个项目；**Small batch** 团队做一组 1–2 周的小项目，**小项目不单独排期**，由团队自己调配保证周期结束前全部发完。
- **Betting table 参会者**：CEO（产品最终拍板）、CTO、一名资深程序员、一名产品策略（Singer 本人）。因为 C-level 时间只能小块拿，会议有「不浪费时间」的气氛，通常一到两小时。**输出是 cycle plan**。最高层在场且没有「第二步审批」，之后没人能插手或打断已排期工作。这套高层买断是周期能正常转起来的关键。
- **bet 的三重含义**：① 有**回报**（不是拿任务填满时间盒，而是明确一个值得下的回报）② 是**承诺**（六周全时独占、零打断，不优化程序员的每小时）③ **下行有上限**（最多亏六周）。
- **Uninterrupted time**：一旦有人来打断，「我们就不再是给了团队六周去做为六周而 shaping 的工作」。动量是**二阶量**（像增长或加速度），不能用单点描述，需要不间断的点序列。「丢掉对的那一小时能毁掉一天；丢掉一天能毁掉一周。」
- **Circuit breaker**：团队必须在赌注时间内发版，**默认不延期**。三条好处：① 消除 runaway project（若只值六周，花两三四五倍是愚蠢的）② 若没做完说明 **shaping 出了问题**，应回到 shaping 轨重做方案而非在坏方案上加时间 ③ 促使团队真正对项目负起所有权，主动做取舍。
- **Bug 怎么办**：**拒绝「bug 天然比别的都重要」的假设**。crisis 很少（数据丢失、全站瘫痪、大量客户看到错误内容才 drop everything）。三种策略：① 用 cool-down 修 ② 太大就拿到 betting table 竞争资源（**「延迟别的工去修 bug」与「事先决定这个 bug 值这个时间」有巨大差别**）③ 每年一次 **bug smash**（通常在假期）。
- **Keep the slate clean**：每次周期清空、只赌一个周期、绝不把旧工作残渣不经过重新 shaping 就带过来。**即使预想某个功能要两个周期，也仍然一次只赌六周**——把第一个六周 shaping 成「有完整可运行的东西」，然后再决定是否续赌。

### 2.9 Ch.9 Place Your Bets

URL: https://basecamp.com/shapeup/2.3-chapter-09 ｜ **[一手]**

> "On an existing product, all of the existing code and design that _isn't_ going to change defines a kind of empty space that the new feature will fit into. Shaping and building is like crafting a piece of furniture for a house that is already built."
> "Whereas adding to an existing product is like buying a couch for a room with fixed dimensions, new product development is like figuring out where the walls and the foundation should go so the building will stand."
> **R&D mode**："we can't reliably shape what we want in advance... We have to learn what we want by building it." / "The aim is to spike, not to ship."
> **Production mode**："Shipping means merging into the main codebase and expecting not to touch it again."
> **Cleanup mode**："In the final phase before launching the new product, we throw all structure out the window. ... It's a free-for-all." / "Cleanup shouldn't last longer than two cycles."
> "There's something about putting your finger near the launch button that makes your hair stand up."
> "A smaller surface area on a V1 means fewer questions to answer, less to support, and less we're committing to maintain indefinitely."
> "Time should always be used strategically."

**中文要点**：
- **既存产品**：固定的代码与设计定义了「新功能要嵌进去的空位」——像给已建好的房子做家具。
- **新产品三阶段**（仍然一次只赌一个周期）：
  - **R&D mode**：赌的是**时间**而非 well-shaped pitch；由资深人员组队（DHH 写代码 + Jason 或资深设计师做设计）；**周期末不期待发布**，目标是 spike 与学习。
  - **Production mode**：核心架构已定，回到标准的 shape→bet→build；团队不再限于资深组；**「发布」= 合入主代码库且预期不再动它**；因为还没对客户开放，仍保有「最终裁剪时删功能」的选项。
  - **Cleanup mode**：**不 shaping、无清晰团队边界、以尽可能小的批次持续合入**；领导层全程掌舵、砍干扰。**最长不超过两个周期。** 同时是领导层做「final cut」决策的阶段。纪律仍重要——要自查是在做 must-have，还是「发版前怯场」在拖延。
- **HEY 案例**：2020 年上线；开发两年，第一年 R&D mode（Jason/David/Jonas 三人），随后近一年 production mode 周期，最后两个 cleanup 周期并大幅裁功能，2020 年 7 月发布。**「每一次对 HEY 的下注都是一次一个下的」**——betting table 在最初几个 R&D 周期并不知道会做 HEY 两年；Basecamp 与 HEY 之间来回切换的能力一直保留。
- **Hill Charts 案例**（灰区）：现有产品上的实验性功能，太冒险不敢直接对客户发布，于是**当作新产品 production mode 来赌**，只做一个「够自己用」的版本；赌一个周期而非两个。
- **betting table 上的五个问题**：Does the problem matter? / Is the appetite right? / Is the solution attractive? / Is this the right time? / Are the right people available?

### 2.10 Ch.10 Hand Over Responsibility

URL: https://basecamp.com/shapeup/3.1-chapter-10 ｜ **[一手]**

> "We _don't_ start by assigning tasks to anyone. Nobody plays the role of the 'taskmaster' or the 'architect' who splits the project up into pieces for other people to execute."
> **"Splitting the project into tasks up front is like putting the pitch through a paper shredder. Everybody just gets disconnected pieces."**
> "Talented people don't like being treated like 'code monkeys' or ticket takers."
> "Planning up front makes you blind to the reality along the way."
> **"Done means deployed"** — "At the end of the cycle, the team will deploy their work."
> "That also means any testing and QA needs to happen _within_ the cycle."
> "Work in the first few days doesn't look like 'work.' No one is checking off tasks. Nothing is getting deployed. ... There can be an odd kind of radio silence."
> "Interfering or asking them for status too early hurts the project. It takes away time that the team needs to find the best approach. The exploration needs to happen anyway. Asking for visible progress will only push it underground."
> "Generally speaking, if the silence doesn't start to break after three days, that's a reasonable time to step in and see what's going on."
> **"The way to really figure out what needs to be done is to start doing real work."**

**中文要点**：
- **分配项目，不分配任务**——预先拆任务是「把 pitch 送进碎纸机」；要项目在整个过程中保持「完整」。团队自己定义任务与方法，拥有完全自主权。
- **"Done means deployed"**——周期末部署；Small Batch 团队自行安排小项目的发布节奏，但都要在周期结束前完成。**测试与 QA 必须在周期内完成**（团队靠缩 scope、早完成核心部分、提前协调 QA 来腾出空间）。帮助文档、营销更新、对客户公告**通常不算在内**（风险薄尾，多在 cool-down 做）。
- **Kick-off**：新建 Basecamp 项目 → 把 shaped concept（原 pitch 或精简版）贴到 Message Board → 在 chat room 约一个 kick-off call 答疑。
- **Getting oriented**——头几天「不像工作」：没人勾任务、没东西部署、没交付物、团队之间也很少沟通，近乎无线电静默。每个人都在搞清现有系统怎么运作、从哪里切入。**管理者必须尊重这个阶段**；过早要状态会「把探索推入地下」。**三天**是合理的介入时间点。
- **Imagined vs discovered tasks**——团队一开始有的是「想象的任务」（光想想就知道要做的），真正的大头是动手后**发现**的任务。**「真正搞清要做什么的办法就是开始做真活。」**

### 2.11 Ch.11 Get One Piece Done

URL: https://basecamp.com/shapeup/3.2-chapter-11 ｜ **[一手]**

> "A team can do a lot of work but feel insecure because they don't have anything real to show for it yet. Lots of things are done but nothing is _really_ done."
> "Instead they should aim to make something tangible and demoable early—in the first week or so. That requires integrating vertically on one small piece of the project instead of chipping away at the horizontal layers."
> "Meanwhile, the programmer wasn't waiting around. He had enough guidance from the pitch to start spiking the access model."
> "Programmers don't need a pixel-perfect design to start implementing. All they need are endpoints: input elements, buttons, places where stored data should appear. These affordances are the core of a user interface design."
> "Beautiful alignment, colors, and typography don't matter on the first pass. Visual styling is important in the end product, not in the early stages. ... **First make it work, then make it beautiful.**"
> "The point is to create a back-and-forth between design and programming on the same piece of the product. Instead of one big hand-off, take turns layering in affordances, code, and visual styling."
> "They didn't build a way to create an interview project and an interview subject before solving the problem of adding interview data. They jumped straight into the middle where the interesting problem was and stubbed everything else to get there."
> "First, it should be **core**. ... Second, it should be **small**. ... Third, it should be **novel**."

**中文要点**：
- **一张完整切片**——别造「第 11 小时才拼装」的主计划。团队可能勾掉很多任务却「没有一件是真做完的」，于是不安全感上升。目标是**第一周左右就做出可点击可演示的东西**，办法是**垂直整合一小片**，而不是横向削层。
- **前/后端分离的两种失败**：只有前端 → nothing works；只有后端 → nothing works。要的是**在两层上圈出同一块区域**，做出「something works」。
- **Case study: Clients in Projects**（Basecamp 3）——1 设计 + 1 程序。设计师选**可见性开关**作为第一个整合点（最核心的 UI、demo 视频里会出现、客户最常用）。他不做像素级 mockup，而是在 HTML 模板里试不同 affordance 与位置。程序员没等——靠 pitch 的指引先去 spike 访问模型。设计师觉得方向 OK 就 ping 程序员，程序员把开关接上（能出现在所有内容类型、能点击切状态、能存库）。此时开关**还不真正改变可见性**，但从服务方的视角可用。**项目开始约三天后**设计师向经理演示，几轮微调后这个 scope 被判定「done」。
- **Affordances before pixel-perfect screens**——程序员只需要 endpoint（输入元素、按钮、数据显示位置）。字体/颜色/间距/布局可以之后解决。文案 + 基本 affordance + 一些接线，就够跑一个活的浏览器版本，从而**早期回答根本问题**：讲得通吗？看得懂吗？是我们想要的吗？
- **Program just enough for the next step**——后端可以有策略地「斑驳」：有 controller 没 model，或有 controller 和带 mock 数据的一点点 model 但不支持增改；未接线的屏幕至少用路由连起来便于导航。案例：访谈 App 要装真实敏感数据，团队**不**做完整用户名密码，直接用 HTTPAuth 硬编码一个密码——「不为了学不到东西的认证代码减速」。
- **Start in the middle**——不先做登录、不先做创建项目，直奔「有趣的问题」所在的中段，其余全部 stub。选第一块的三条标准：**core（核心）/ small（小）/ novel（新）**。若两块都 core 且 small，优先选**从没做过的**——因为那才能消除不确定性。反例：新增客户的 UI 与新增普通用户的 UI 大同小异，先做它不会学到任何东西。

### 2.12 Ch.12 Map the Scopes

URL: https://basecamp.com/shapeup/3.3-chapter-12 ｜ **[一手]**

> "When asked to organize tasks for a project, people often separate work by person or role: they'll create a list for Designers and a list for Programmers. This leads to the problem we talked about in the previous chapter—people will complete tasks, but the tasks won't add up to a finished part of the project early enough."
> "Instead, they should create lists based on the _structure_ of the project—the things that can be worked on and finished independently of each other."
> "We call these integrated slices of the project `scopes`. We break the overall scope (singular) of the project into separate scopes (plural) that can be finished independently of each other."
> **"Scope mapping isn't planning. You need to walk the territory before you can draw the map."**
> "Scopes properly drawn are not arbitrary groupings or categories for the sake of tidiness. They reflect the real ground truth of what can be done independently."
> "That's why at the start of a project, we don't expect to see accurate scopes. We're more likely to see them at the end of week one or start of week two."
> "Scopes are more than just slices. They become the language of the project at the macro level."
> "When you feel a pain in your body, you don't have to question whether it's in your arms or your legs or your head. You know the parts and their names so you can explain where the pain is."
> "We allow ourselves a 'Chowder' list for loose tasks that don't fit anywhere. But we always keep a skeptical eye on it. If it gets longer than three to five items, something is fishy."
> "In a world with no deadlines, we could improve everything forever. But in a fixed time box, we need a machete in our hands."

**中文要点**：
- **按结构组织，不按人组织**——「Designers 清单 / Programmers 清单」会导致「任务都完成了，但凑不出一个完成的部分」。应按**项目结构**（可独立推进与完成的东西）建清单。软件项目没有预切好的类别，每个项目都是要走一遍才能画出地图的野地。
- **Scope 定义（官方 Glossary）**："Parts of a project that can be built, integrated, and finished independently of the rest of the project." 集成前端与后端任务，能「确凿地完成并翻篇」。
- **Scope map 的四个演进画面**：轮廓（只有 shaping 留下的外框）→ 满天任务点（太底层，高空看不见东西）→ 划出边界如地图上的州 → 每个 scope = 一个 to-do list（list 名即 scope 名）。
- **Scope mapping 不是 planning**——先走地形再画地图。所以**项目开头不该期待准确的 scope**；通常在**第一周末或第二周初**才浮现。初期有反复改名/重划是正常的。
- **判断 scope 画对了的三个迹象**：① 感觉能看到整个项目，没有让你担忧的重要东西藏在细节里 ② 关于项目的对话变得更流畅（scope 给了正确的语言）③ 新任务出现时你知道该放哪儿。
- **该重画的三个迹象**：① 说不清一个 scope 有多「done」（内部任务互不相关）② 名字不专属于本项目（如「front-end」「bugs」——被称作 **grab bags / junk drawers**）③ 太大、太快完成不了。
- **三种特殊形态**：
  - **Layer cake**（官方定义为 "A scope of work you can estimate by looking at the surface area of the UI."）：UI 设计 + 薄而均匀分布的后端，设计任务与程序任务可放在同一个 scope。是多数「信息系统」类应用的合理默认。
  - **Iceberg**（官方定义为 "A scope of work where the back-end work is much more complex than the UI or vice versa."）：前后端复杂度严重失衡；把 UI 拆成单独 scope，必要时把复杂后端再拆成多个 concern。也有**倒冰山**（UI 复杂度极高、后端简单，如多天事件跨格换行的日历）。**无论哪种，都要先反问其存在性**：这个复杂度真的必要且不可约吗？需要那么花哨的 UI 吗？后端有没有别的做法可以减少与系统其余部分的依赖？
  - **Chowder**：装不进任何 scope 的零散任务。保持在 3–5 条以内，超了说明有问题。
- **~ 标记 nice-to-have**——新任务不断涌现（可清理的代码、边界情况、既有功能改进）。把它们记在 scope 里但前面加 `~`，让全队持续区分 must-haves 与 nice-to-haves。整个 scope 也可以标 `~`。

### 2.13 Ch.13 Show Progress

URL: https://basecamp.com/shapeup/3.4-chapter-13 ｜ **[一手]**

> "Good-hearted managers don't like asking for status. It's awkward, feels like nagging."
> "In real life, issues are discovered by getting involved in the problem. That means **_to-do lists actually grow as the team makes progress_**."
> "The problem with estimates is they have a very different meaning depending on the nature of the work being estimated. ... It's not meaningful to write '4 hours, or maybe 3 days' as the estimate."
> "Recognizing this, we came up with a way to see the status of the project without counting tasks and without numerical estimates. We do that by shifting the focus from what's done or not done to what's unknown and what's solved. To enable this shift, we use the metaphor of the hill."
> **"Every piece of work has two phases. First there's the uphill phase of figuring out what our approach is and what we're going to do. Then, once we can see all the work involved, there's the downhill phase of execution."**
> "Note how the hill shows how the work _feels_ at different stages. The uphill phase is full of uncertainty, unknowns, and problem solving. The downhill phase is marked by certainty, confidence, seeing everything, and knowing what to do."
> "A dot that doesn't move is effectively a raised hand: 'Something might be wrong here.'"
> "The hill chart allows everybody to see that somebody might be stuck without them actually saying it."
> "It's less about the person (Looks like you're stuck!) and more about the work. The question is: What can we solve to get that over the hill?"
> "Some teams struggle with backsliding when they first try the hill chart. ... it's often because somebody did the uphill work with their head instead of their hands. ... It's good to think of the first third uphill as 'I've thought about this,' the second third as 'I've validated my approach,' and the final third to the top as 'I'm far enough with what I've built that I don't believe there are other unknowns.'"
> **"That motivates the team to push the scariest work uphill first."**
> "Work expands to fill the time available."
> "Journalists have a concept called the 'inverted pyramid.' ... Effective teams sequence their problem solving in the same way."

**中文要点**：
- **任务清单为什么不足以判断进度**：清单可能显示「全部完成」但其实只是「还没定义任务」；一个 scope 可能早早建了但还没填任务；QA 阶段测试动作本身会给 scope 添加新任务。**to-do list 实际上会随着进度增长**（书中有 "In Theory" vs "In Practice" 对照图）。
- **估算为什么不显示不确定性**：两个都估 4 小时的任务，一个是做过十遍的、一个是没做过的，后者可能是 3 天；写「4 小时，或者可能 3 天」没有意义。
- **Hill chart**（官方定义："A diagram showing the status of work on a spectrum from unknown to known to done."）——把焦点从「做完没做完」转到「**什么是未知的、什么是已解决的**」。
- **山的隐喻**：上坡 = 搞清方法（充满不确定、未知、解题）；下坡 = 执行（确定、有信心、看得见全貌、知道要做什么）。书里的晚饭派对例子：从「还没开始想」→「选菜系（山腰）」→「定菜谱+列购物清单（山顶，此时才能合理估时）」→ 买食材 → 做饭 → 清理。
- **Scopes on the hill**——scope 提供项目的**语言**（"Locate"、"Reply"），hill 描述每个 scope 的**状态**（uphill/downhill）。用不同颜色把 scope 点在山图上。
- **Status without asking**——Basecamp 内有 hill chart 功能，团队成员自己拖点更新，日志记录在项目上。**对管理者而言「比较历史状态」是杀手功能**：不只看工作在哪，而是看工作**如何移动**。这个报告成为管理者焦虑时的第一去处，**不用打断团队问尴尬的状态问题**；发现异常可以直接跳到具体工作对话：「看起来 'Autosave' 上坡很久了，卡住它的未知是什么？」
- **Nobody says "I don't know"**——没人愿意向管理层举手说「我不知道怎么解」。这会**让团队隐藏不确定性、累积风险**；而卡住/打转的时刻正是最大风险与机会所在。hill chart 让「可能卡住了」**不用开口就能被看见**：**不动的点就是举起来的手**。已经有 uphill/downhill 语言后，对话从「你卡住了？」转向「我们要解决什么才能把它推过山头？」
- **Prompts to refactor the scopes**——有时「卡住」不是真卡住，而是 scope 划错了。案例：`Notify` scope 六天不动，查下去发现它其实是三件事（设计邮件、后端投递、应用内菜单显示）。拆成 `Email` / `Hey Menu` / `Deliver` 三个 scope 后，**二阶收益**出现：三个 scope 能独立移动，团队能更频繁地展示进展。
- **Build your way uphill**——「回滑」通常是因为**用脑子而不是用手**做上坡工作（「我用那个 API 就行了」→ 现实更复杂）。上坡三段式的自我校准说法：**「我想过了」→「我验证了方法」→「我建到足够远，不再相信还有别的未知」**。
- **Solve in the right sequence**——先把**最吓人的工作**推上坡（geocoding vs 邮件模板：邮件模板可以最后一周一天搞定，geocoder 可能困住团队数周）。把关键 scope 早推过山头，**拧螺丝的事留到后面**。书中引用新闻业的「**倒金字塔**」结构：最重要的信息放最前，细节按重要性递减排列，这样排版时可以砍尾巴而不丢关键内容。

### 2.14 Ch.14 Decide When to Stop

URL: https://basecamp.com/shapeup/3.5-chapter-14 ｜ **[一手]**

> "Shipping on time means shipping something imperfect. There's always some queasiness in the stomach as you look at your work and ask yourself: Is it good enough?"
> **"It helps to shift the point of comparison. Instead of comparing up against the ideal, compare down to `baseline`—the current reality for customers."**
> "This motivates us to make calls on the things that are slowing us down. It's less about us and more about value for the customer. It's the difference between 'never good enough' and 'better than what they have now.'"
> "This forces the team to make trade-offs. ... Without a deadline, they could easily delay the project for changes that don't actually deserve the extra time."
> **"Scope grows naturally. Scope creep isn't the fault of bad clients, bad managers, or bad programmers."**
> "Every project is full of scope we don't need. Every part of a product doesn't need to be equally prominent, equally fast, and equally polished."
> **"Rather than trying to stop scope from growing, give teams the tools, authority, and responsibility to constantly cut it down."**
> **"Cutting scope isn't lowering quality"** ... "Variable scope is not about sacrificing quality. We are extremely picky about the quality of our code, our visual design, the copy in our interfaces, and the performance of our interactions."
> "People often talk about 'cutting' scope. We use an even stronger word—`hammering`—to reflect the power and force it takes to repeatedly bang the scope so it fits in the time box."
> "QA generates `discovered tasks` that are all `nice-to-haves` by default."
> "Therefore we think of QA as a level-up, not a gate or a check-point that all work must go through."

**中文要点**：
- **Compare to baseline**——设计师和程序员总想做到最好（按钮在首屏还是设置页两页之后，都一样用心；程序员希望代码库自洽、边界全覆）。**自豪感要用对靶子**。办法是**把比较方向掉转：不向上比理想，向下比 baseline（客户当前的现实）**。客户今天怎么解决这个问题？这个功能消除了什么恼人的绕路？「永远不够好」→「比他们现在用的好」。
- **Limits motivate trade-offs**——六周赌注带 circuit breaker，逼出取舍。有人问「如果……会不会更好」或发现新边界情况时，先自问：**有时间吗？**
- **Scope grows like grass**——scope 自然生长；**scope creep 不是坏客户、坏经理或坏程序员的错**。项目在宏观尺度上是不透明的，不钻进去看不到微观细节。「与其试图阻止 scope 生长，不如给团队工具、权力和责任去不断砍它。」
- **Cutting scope isn't lowering quality**——做选择不是留洞，而是让产品**在某些方面更好**；对 scope 挑剔**使产品差异化**。可变 scope 不等于牺牲质量：37signals 对代码质量、视觉设计、界面文案、交互性能都极为挑剔；诀窍是问**哪些事真正重要、哪些能动指针、哪些对核心用例有影响**。
- **Scope hammering**（官方定义："Forcefully questioning a design, implementation, or use case to cut scope and finish inside the fixed time box."）——书里给了 **8 个自查问题**（见 Ch.14 原文）：是否 must-have？不发布它行不行？不做会怎样？这是新问题还是客户早已忍受的老问题？这个情况发生的可能性多大？发生时哪些客户看到——是所有人用的核心，还是边缘情况？真发生时的实际影响有多大？当某个用例表现不佳时，该用例与我们目标受众的契合度如何？
- **Must-haves vs nice-to-haves**——must-have 记在 scope 上，未完成前 scope 不算 done；nice-to-have（前缀 `~`）可以在 scope 判 done 后继续挂着，有时间就做、没时间就砍——**「通常它们永远不会被做」**。Singer 说：**把它们标成 nice-to-have 的这个动作本身就是 scope hammering。**
- **QA is for the edges**——Basecamp 当前规模（数百万用户、产品团队约十几人）**只有一名 QA**，在周期后段介入，专猎核心功能之外的边缘情况。QA 能只看边缘，是因为设计师和程序员对基础质量负责（程序员自己写测试）。**QA 是「升级」而不是「闸门或检查点」**——有 QA 比没有好得多，但不依赖 QA 来发布可用的功能。**代码审查同理**：团队不必等 code review 就能发布，没有正式检查点；有时间和必要时资深者看代码给反馈，更像教学机会而非流程必经步骤。
- **When to extend a project**（极少数情况延两周）：两个条件——① 未完成的必须是**真正的 must-haves**，且已经受过所有 scope hammer 的考验；② 未完成的工作必须**全部在下坡**（没有未解问题、没有开放疑问）。**任何周期末的上坡工作都指向 shaping 疏漏或概念有洞**——未知太多不足以继续下注，应下周期做别的、把项目退回 shaping 阶段。即使条件满足，也**仍倾向于守纪律、执行 appetite**；两周 cool-down 通常已提供足够缓冲。**「滑进 cool-down」不该成为习惯——它要么指向 shaping 流程问题，要么指向团队绩效问题。**

### 2.15 Ch.15 Move On

URL: https://basecamp.com/shapeup/3.6-chapter-15 ｜ **[一手]**

> "Shipping can actually generate new work if you're not careful. Feature releases beget feature requests."
> "A small minority of customers might overreact and say things like 'You ruined it! Change it back!'"
> "It's important to stay cool and avoid knee-jerk reactions. Give it a few days and allow it to die down."
> "It can be tempting to commit to making changes in response to feedback, but then you no longer have a clean slate for the next cycle. Remember: these are just `raw ideas` coming in. The way to handle them is with a gentle 'no.'"
> **"Saying 'no' doesn't prevent you from continuing to contemplate them and maybe shape them up into future projects. Saying 'yes,' on the other hand, takes away your freedom in the future. It's like taking on debt."**
> "Remember, the thing you just shipped was a six-week `bet`. If this part of the product needs more time, then it requires a new bet."
> "Here we come full circle. The raw ideas that just came in from customer feedback aren't actionable yet. They need to be shaped."

**中文要点**：
- **Let the storm pass**——发布本身会催生新工作（新需求、bug、改进建议）。若改动触及既有工作流，或哪怕只是纯视觉改动，都可能招来激烈反弹（少数客户会说「你毁了它！改回去！」）。**保持冷静、别膝跳反射，给它几天时间平息**；对为什么改、改了对谁有益要坚定。
- **Stay debt-free**——承诺按反馈改，就失去了下周期的干净石板。这些都只是 raw ideas，用柔和的「不」应对。**说「不」不阻止你继续琢磨、将来把它 shaped 成项目；说「是」则夺走你未来的自由——像负债。** 刚发布的是一次六周赌注；这部分产品需要更多时间就是**需要一次新的赌注**，让这些请求或 bug 去 betting table 与别的东西竞争。
- **Feedback needs to be shaped**——回到起点：客户反馈来的 raw ideas 还不可执行，它们正是 shaping 第一步（Set Boundaries）的原材料。若某个请求真的重要，可以在**下个周期的 shaping 轨上把它列为头号优先**，同时给团队赌别的项目去建；六周后再在 betting table 上论证、排期 shaped 版本。

### 2.17 Appendix: Adjust to Your Size

URL: https://basecamp.com/shapeup/4.1-appendix-02 ｜ **[一手]**

> **"To apply Shape Up to your company, it helps to separate out the basic truths from the specific practices."**
> "Work has to come from somewhere, and it takes work to figure out what the right work is. This is shaping."
> **"If we don't make trade-offs up front by shaping, the universe will force us to make trade-offs later in a mad rush when we're confronted by deadlines, technical limitations, or resource constraints."**
> "Six weeks might not be the exact time frame for your team. But the consequences of making unclear or open-ended commitments are the same for everyone."
> "These truths apply regardless of the size of your organization. The specific practices, on the other hand, are scale-dependent."
> **Small enough to wing it**："a tiny team can throw out most of the structure. You don't need to work six weeks at a time. You don't need a cool-down period, formal pitches or a betting table."
> **Big enough to specialize**："After you hire more people, all of this fluidity flips from an asset to a liability. Winging it with ad-hoc meetings and chat room discussions doesn't work anymore."
> "At Basecamp's current size (about 50 people in the whole company, roughly a dozen in the product team)..."

**中文要点**：
- **基本真理 vs 具体做法**——这是全附录最有价值的一条切分：
  - **真理**（与规模无关）：工作总得有个来源，而搞清「什么是对的工作」本身就要花功夫（这就是 shaping）；shaping 设定更清晰的边界与预期；**如果不事先通过 shaping 做取舍，宇宙会逼你在截止日、技术限制、资源约束撞上来时手忙脚乱地补做取舍**；下注这件事上，时限具体是几周不重要，但「承诺含糊/开放式的后果对所有人都一样」；要区分已知与未知以便正确排序。
  - **具体做法**（随规模变化）：六周、cool-down、正式 pitch、betting table、双轨制。
- **规模小到可以临场发挥**：2–3 人时人人身兼数职，很难承诺长块的不间断时间；同时沟通与掉头更容易。**可以扔掉大部分结构**——不必六周一段、不必 cool-down、不必正式 pitch 与 betting table。同一批人来回切换帽子即可，但**要有意识地明确自己现在戴哪顶帽子、处于哪个阶段**：设 appetite → shape → build → shape 下一个。赌注大小可以每次不同（这次两周、那次三周）。**shaping / betting / building 三个阶段依然成立，只是没有周期与 cool-down 的刚性结构。**
- **大到需要专业化**：人一多，「临场发挥」从资产变成负债；协调吃掉时间、事情开始漏掉。此时才该上六周周期 + cool-down + 正式 betting table。**需要有人的角色从「周期内做设计」上移到「周期外做 shaping」**。
- 当前 Basecamp 约 50 人（产品团队约 12 人），靠角色专业化让设计与程序团队能在周期内不受打扰：**SIP 团队**（Security, Infrastructure, Performance）处理底层技术工作，**Ops 团队**维持运转，**Support 团队里的技术人员**可以调查客户提出的问题——因此不需要打断 Core Product 团队。

### 2.18 Appendix: How to Begin to Shape Up

URL: https://basecamp.com/shapeup/4.2-appendix-03 ｜ **[一手]**

> **Option A: One six-week experiment** — "You don't need to change everything all at once."
> "Shape one significant project that can be comfortably finished within six weeks. Be conservative and allow extra time on your first run."
> "Carve out one designer and two programmers' time for the entire six weeks. Guarantee that nobody will interrupt them."
> "You don't need to worry about Mapping the Scopes or Showing Progress right away. You should see a big leap in progress just by dedicating uninterrupted time, shaping the work in advance, and letting the team work out the details."
> **Option B: Start with shaping** — 若 CTO 控制程序员时间无法凑齐六周，就先 shaping 一个边界更清晰的项目，走公司现有排期流程（**"even if it's a paper shredder"**）。
> **Option C: Start with cycles** — 对原两周 sprint 的团队，先切到六周周期，去掉持续开规划会的开销。
> **"Fix shipping first"** — "Build your shipping muscles before you worry too much about improving your research or discovery process. You can have the best customer insight in the world, but if you can't turn it into a project and ship, it won't matter."
> **"Focus on the end result"** — "Ask yourself: How will we feel if we ship this project after six weeks? Will we feel good about what we accomplished? ... It's the outcome that matters."

**中文要点**：
- **不要一次全改**。**Option A 六周实验**的六步：① shape 一个六周内能舒服做完的项目（**首次要做保守、留额外时间**）② 划出 1 设计 + 2 程序的整六周，**保证全程无人打断** ③ 不做正式 betting table，直接安排团队做这个实验 ④ kick-off 时用完整 pitch 配料呈现 shaped work，**明确告知团队任务由他们自己发现和跟踪** ⑤ 给这个跨职能团队一个实体空间或聊天室 ⑥ 鼓励他们**尽早把 UI 与代码接线**（Get One Piece Done）。
- **先别管 scope mapping 与 hill chart**——只要做到「不间断时间 + 事先 shaping + 让团队自己搞定细节」，就能看到巨大跃迁。等团队习惯 Get One Piece Done，再上 scope mapping；scope 定义得好之后，再上 hill chart 展示进度。
- **Option B 从 shaping 入手**——即使现有排期流程是「碎纸机」，更好的 shaped work 也能照亮工程团队，让他们对更长周期或更慎重的 betting 流程开眼。
- **Option C 从周期入手**——对原用两周 sprint 的团队，先切六周能去掉持续规划会开销，给程序员更多积累动量的时间。
- **Fix shipping first**——先练出「发布肌肉」，再操心研究/发现流程。
- **Focus on the end result**——把心态从微观切到宏观：六周后发布这个项目，我们会感觉良好吗？「进度是按小时或天的尺度上具体发生了什么并不重要，重要的是结果。」

### 5.3 《It Doesn't Have to Be Crazy at Work》的一手补充：Seven Shipping Principles

来源：https://basecamp.com/guides/seven-shipping-principles ｜ **[一手]**

官方的七条「发布原则」，其中第 7 条直接承接 Shape Up 的 appetite：

> "**We ship to our appetite.** One of Shape Up's key concepts is the appetite. Projects are kicked off on a premise that they're only worth doing if a good version of their pitch can be done within 2, 4, or 6 weeks. Like a great company can be a terrible stock at an exorbitant price, so too can a great pitch become a mistake if it's pursued far beyond the appetite."
> "Constraints force us to make choices and rank what we'd rather have if it's 'or' not 'and'. ... What we usually need are substitutions, not additions."
> "Feature creep and blown estimates are the industry standard. Our standard is that we ship the best work within the time we've given it, and we hold our heads proud to the compromises that entail."

另外值得一提的一手细节（第 1 条 `We only ship good work`）：
> "We intentionally constrain ourselves through the cycles in Shape Up, such that we don't end up spending 2 months on stuff that warranted 2 weeks worth of work. Not every batch of work is going to be a 10/10. But if it's less than 8/10, it probably shouldn't go out the door. If it's less than a 7/10, there's no way it should go out the door, except as an emergency patch you immediately return to cleanup."

**⚠️ 矛盾点**：这里 37signals 用「8/10 才能出门」的**质量分档制**，而 Shape Up Ch.14 明确说 **"Cutting scope isn't lowering quality"**、要「向下比 baseline」。两者可以调和（一个是「不砍质量」，一个是「砍 scope」），但**措辞上确实存在张力**：Shape Up 反对用「理想完美度」做标尺，而 Seven Shipping Principles 引入了 8/10、10/10 这种与理想比较的打分。这一矛盾予以保留，见第 8 节。

### 7.6 【关键发现】Getting Real（2006）已包含 Shape Up 的三大支柱

本次抓取发现：**Shape Up 被广泛认为是「新方法论」的几个核心概念，早在 Getting Real 里就以成章的形式存在了。** 这对「哪些是 Ryan Singer 原创、哪些是 37signals 的既有资产」的归属判断至关重要。

#### ① 「固定时间、可变 scope」的原始表述 —— Getting Real Ch.7

URL: https://basecamp.com/gettingreal/02.4-fix-time-and-budget-flex-scope ｜ **[一手]**

> 「**Fix Time and Budget, Flex Scope**」
> "Here's an easy way to launch on time and on budget: keep them fixed. **Never throw more time or money at a problem, just scale back the scope.**"
> "There's a myth that goes like this: we can launch on time, on budget, and on scope. It almost never happens and when it does quality often suffers."
> "If you can't fit everything in within the time and budget allotted then don't expand the time and budget. Instead, **pull back the scope**. There's always time to add stuff later — **later is eternal, now is fleeting**."
> "Our recommendation: **Scope down.** It's better to make half a product than a half-assed product."

**对照**：这与 Shape Up Ch.3 的 "fixed time, variable scope" 与 Signal 24「Fixed」是**同一个原则的三种表述形式**（2006 成章 → 2019 术语化 → 现行一句话版）。**「Fixed time, variable scope」这个时髦术语是 Singer 的命名，但原则本身是 37signals 2006 年就有的。**

#### ② 「先说不」的原始表述 —— Getting Real Ch.23

URL: https://basecamp.com/gettingreal/05.3-start-with-no ｜ **[一手]**

> "**The secret to building half a product instead of a half-ass product is saying no.** Each time you say yes to a feature, you're adopting a child."
> "Make each feature work hard to be implemented. ... You should only consider features if they're willing to stand on the porch for three days waiting to be let in."
> "**That's why you start with no.** Every new feature request that comes to us — or from us — meets a no. We listen but don't act. The initial response is 'not now.' **If a request for a feature keeps coming back, that's when we know it's time to take a deeper look.**"
> 章末引用 Derek Sivers（CD Baby）转述 Steve Jobs：
> "I know you have a thousand ideas for all the cool features iTunes could have. So do we. **But we don't want a thousand features.** That would be ugly. Innovation is not about saying yes to everything. **It's about saying NO to all but the most crucial features.**"

**对照**：Shape Up Ch.3 的 "Our default response to any idea that comes up should be: 'Interesting. Maybe some day.'" + Ch.15 的 "a gentle 'no'" + Ch.7「重要想法会自己回来」，是这条 2006 年原则的直接延续。Signal 21「Know no」与 Rework 的「Say no by default」同源。**注意**：网传的「Say no by default」其实出自 **Derek Sivers 2004 年的博客标题**（Getting Real 引用其为来源，原始链接已死，见上表 web.archive.org 存档 URL），**不是 Rework 的原创章名** —— 见 8.8。

#### ③ 「小团队 / 沟通路径指数增长」—— 找到「不能中途加人」的最近一手证据

**这是本次对用户点名问题的关键补正。** Getting Real Ch.38 章末引用 **The Ganssle Group** 的一段（[P48](https://basecamp.com/gettingreal/07.3-meetings-are-toxic)）—— **[一手]**（37signals 官方收录并背书）：

> "As projects grow, **adding people has a diminishing return**. One of the most interesting reasons is the increased number of communications channels. Two people can only talk to each other; there's only a single comm path. Three workers have three communications paths; 4 have 6. In fact, **the growth of links is exponential**…Pretty soon memos and meetings eat up the entire work day."
> "**The solution is clear: break teams into smaller, autonomous and independent units to reduce these communications links.**"

Getting Real Ch.7 章末还引用了 **Fred Brooks《The Mythical Man-Month》**—— **[一手]**（官方收录）：

> "How does a project get to be a year behind schedule? **One day at a time.**"
> — Fred Brooks, software engineer and computer scientist (出自 *The Mythical Man-Month*)

**结论修正**：用户在任务里点名的「不能中途加人」——**《Shape Up》正文中确实没有找到独立论述**（见 8.7），**但这条思想的最近官方出处是 Getting Real**，并且 37signals 在那里就**明确引用过《人月神话》与「沟通路径指数增长」论证**。所以正确表述是：**「加人边际收益递减」是 37signals 自 Getting Real 起就承接的既有信念（源头是 Fred Brooks），Shape Up 只是继承而未展开。** 若后续蒸馏要写「Singer 说不能中途加人」，属于编造；应写「37signals 在 Getting Real 阶段即持此立场」。

#### ④ 「反 spec」的原始表述 —— Getting Real Ch.59

URL: https://basecamp.com/gettingreal/11.1-theres-nothing-functional-about-afunctional-spec ｜ **[一手]**

> "**Don't write a functional specifications document.**"
> "**Functional specs are fantasies.** They don't reflect reality. An app is not real until builders are building it, designers are designing it, and people are using it."
> "**Functional specs only lead to an illusion of agreement.** A bunch of people agreeing on paragraphs of text isn't a true agreement. Everyone may be reading the same thing but they're thinking something different."
> "**Functional specs force you to make the most important decisions when you have the least information.** You know the least about something when you begin to build it. ... That's when you should be making decisions — when you have more information, not less."
> "**Functional specs lead to feature overload.** There's no pushback during spec phase. There's no cost to writing something down and adding another bullet point."
> 替代方案："Go with a briefer alternative that moves you toward something real. Write a one page story about what the app needs to do. ... **If it takes more than a page to explain it, then it's too complex.**"
> "Then begin building the interface — **the interface will be the alternative to the functional spec.** Draw some quick and simple paper sketches. Then start coding it into html."

**对照**：这是 Shape Up Ch.10「Splitting the project into tasks up front is like putting the pitch through a paper shredder」、Ch.11「Affordances before pixel-perfect screens」、Ch.6「pitch 一页故事」的直系祖先。**「幻觉式一致」（illusion of agreement）这个说法 2006 年就出现了**——Jason Fried 2020 年在 SvN 还专门写了一篇《Don't take their word for it》重提它并回溯到本章（见 7.7）。

## 8. 矛盾与不确定的地方（**保留矛盾，不和稀泥**）

### 8.1 【核心矛盾】**Shape Up 是「反敏捷」的，但被广泛当作敏捷流派**

- **一手证据（反对）**：Jason Fried 序言明确写 "we're not into waterfall or agile or scrum... No backlogs, no Kanban, no velocity tracking, none of that."（https://basecamp.com/shapeup/0.1-foreword）
- **一手证据（反 sprint）**：Ch.8 "Some companies use two-week cycles (aka 'sprints'). We learned that two weeks is too short... extremely costly due to the planning overhead."（https://basecamp.com/shapeup/2.2-chapter-08）
- **一手证据（反估时）**：Ch.3 与 Ch.13 均把 estimate 作为对立面。
- **现象**：二手市场普遍把 Shape Up 归入「敏捷方法论」或「Scrum 替代方案」。
- **处理**：保留矛盾。可表述为「**Shape Up 自我定位在 agile/scrum 谱系之外，但在实践中常被作为敏捷的替代或补充被采用**」。

### 8.2 【作者自曝的矛盾】**书里说的是 Basecamp 特例，不是普适方法**

这是本次调研**最重要的发现**，因为它直接限定了后续蒸馏的边界。

**一手证据**：Singer 本人 2025-10-28 文章（https://www.ryansinger.co/pitfalls-when-adopting-shape-up/）：

> "I see some common pitfalls when people try to do Shape Up 'by the book.' There are things in the book that are Basecamp-specific or often misunderstood, and teams who successfully adopt Shape Up all find ways around them."

> **"Pitfall #1: Shaping without technical depth.** The book says shaping is primarily design work. **But everyone at Basecamp — including designers — was very technical!** If you try to shape with only PMs and non-technical designers, projects will churn because of the unanswered questions that blow up during build. **The #1 failure mode of attempted Shape Up adoptions is 'undershaped' work.**"

**这与书中 Ch.2 的表态存在直接张力**：书中 Ch.2 说 "Shaping is primarily design work. ... You don't need to be a programmer to shape, but you need to be technically literate." —— 语气是「技术素养足够」，而五年后 Singer 的回顾是「**必须**有资深技术人员参与 shaping，否则是头号失败模式」。**这是一个真实的自我修正，应予保留，且应视作 Shape Up 实践的权威注解。**

### 8.3 【新增概念，书中不存在】**Framing** 与 **Shape** 的切分

**一手证据**：Singer 2025：
> "There is a distinct work step to nail down the actual problem and outcome before shaping. We didn't have a word for it when I wrote the book. **Now it's called Framing.** (It's implicit in the book. Ch. 3 'Set boundaries' is actually framing. Ch. 4-5 is shaping.)"

并给出了新的标准术语（书中没有）：
> - **Candidate** — A request or idea that hasn't been framed yet.
> - **Frame Go** — Approved to shape. Problem/outcome are tight enough...
> - **Shape Go** — Ready to build. No material unknowns from both a technical and interaction standpoint.

**⚠️ 后续蒸馏的硬约束**：**不得把 "Framing" 当作《Shape Up》书中的概念引用**。它是 Singer 2025 年的事后补充命名。若引用，必须标注为 2025 年作者本人的后续修正。

### 8.4 【书中未解决的问题，2025 年才给答案】**reactive work 与 shaped work 混在一起**

**一手证据**：Singer 2025：
> "Pitfall #3: Mixing in non-project work. If you don't separate out the shaped project work from incidents, urgent issues, etc, the team won't be able to focus. There will be too much WIP all the time..."
> "Project work that relies on third parties is technically reactive work. If you are waiting for a response from someone else, you don't control the cycle schedule. **Better to do that work on a kanban than Shape Up style.**"

**注意**：书中 Ch.8「What about bugs?」给了三种策略（cool-down / betting table / bug smash），但**没有**提出「为响应式工作建立独立产能」这一条。且 Singer 现在建议「依赖第三方的工作用 kanban 做」——**这与 Fried 序言里「No Kanban」的表态构成有趣的张力**。保留。

### 8.5 质量衡量标准的不一致（见 5.3）

- Shape Up Ch.14："Cutting scope isn't lowering quality"；要求**向下比 baseline**，而非向上比理想。
- Seven Shipping Principles："if it's less than 8/10, it probably shouldn't go out the door"——引入了一个**相对于 10/10 理想**的打分。
- **处理**：保留。可能的调和是「不砍质量 ≠ 不设质量门槛」，但两处措辞的分辨率不同，值得在蒸馏时明确区分，不要合并成一句话。

### 8.6 Ch.5 与 2025 年对「rabbit hole」的处理口径有强化

- 书中 Ch.5 的框架是「找洞 → 补洞 → 或把洞划到边界外（out of bounds）→ 或砍掉」。
- Singer 2025："**Rabbit holes are commonly misunderstood. Any rabbit hole that isn't _solved_ during shaping is a time bomb that can churn the project.**"
- **处理**：这是**收紧**，不是矛盾——但措辞强度不同（书里允许「declare out of bounds」作为处理手段之一，2025 年的表述更强调「必须**解决**」）。保留。

### 8.8 【易错点】「Say no by default」的出处不是 Rework

用户任务把「Say no by default」列为《Rework》的代表主张。本次一手抓取显示：

- **Getting Real Ch.23「Start With No」**章末引用的是 **Derek Sivers（CD Baby 创始人）2004 年博客《Say NO by default》** —— 原始 URL `oreillynet.com/onlamp/blog/2004/08/say_no_by_default.html` **已失效**，Getting Real 官方给的是 web.archive.org 存档链接。**[一手]**（37signals 官方引用标注）。
- 因此「Say no by default」这个**短语**在 37signals 语境中最早是「被引用者」，**源头**是 Derek Sivers，不是 Fried/DHH 原创。

> **✅ 本节结论已被后续取证更正（2026-09-17 收尾时）**
>
> 第 9 节子智能体取到 Rework 完整目录后确认：**「Say no by default」确实是 Rework 的篇目**（EVOLUTION 段首篇），原句为：
> - 「**Start getting into the habit of saying no—even to many of your best ideas. Use the power of no to get your priorities straight.**」
> - 「**You rarely regret saying no. But you often wind up regretting saying yes.**」
> - 「**People avoid saying no because confrontation makes them uncomfortable.** But the alternative is even worse. You drag things out, make things complicated, and work on ideas you don't believe in.」
>
> 来源：[一手·书稿，经第三方转录]；后两句经官方播客逐字复述 [一手]。详见 §4.4 与 §10.3。
>
> **正确的表述方式**（下游引用请用这个）：
> - ✅ 「Rework 有一篇叫《Say no by default》」
> - ✅ 「这个短语的源头是 Derek Sivers 2004 年的博客，Getting Real Ch.23 引用过他」
> - ❌ 不要写成「Say no by default 是 Derek Sivers 的说法，与 Rework 无关」
> - ❌ 也不要写成「Derek Sivers 首创于 Rework」

### 8.9 【表面矛盾，需辨明】「不要高保真」vs「用完整产品验伪」

表面上看：
- Shape Up 全程主张**粗糙**（rough sketches、breadboard、fat marker、affordances before pixel-perfect）；
- Jason Fried 2020 年在《Validation is a mirage》里说"**You can't validate a sketch, or a wireframe, or an MVP that isn't the actual product**"，主张"make the whole thing"。

**这不是矛盾，是两个不同维度的「完整」**：
- Shape Up 说的「完整」= **垂直切片**（vertical slice：前后端都通、能点击、能用真实数据跑起来）—— Ch.11 明确要 "working UI and working code"；
- Fried 反对的「切片」= **横向功能切片**（MVP 式的、把产品按功能切一块出来问用户喜不喜欢）。

**依据**：Fried 原文用的是 "slice of pie… It's homogenous"（同质的派切片）——他攻击的是「假设产品同质、可以用一块代表整体」。而 Shape Up Ch.11 的 "one slice" 恰恰是**打通前后端的那一片**，正是 Fried 所说的「真的能用」。两者一致。

**处理**：保留这个「表面矛盾」的说明，因为它极易被误读；蒸馏时若引用，必须带上「vertical vs horizontal slice」的区分。
