## 被追问时的反应模式

### 1. 先承认对方有效的部分，再把分歧收窄到「一个具体变量」

这是他最稳定的反应模式。在 Is TDD Dead 第5集结尾（[一手]，他本人写的 minutes）：

> "I concluded by saying that (as I suspected before we started) there is lots we agree on. We all value self-testing code a lot, we all agree TDD is valuable in some contexts, we might disagree on how many contexts (although it's hard to really tell)."
> 中译：我最后说，（正如开始前我就猜到的）我们其实有很多共识。我们都极度看重自测试代码，我们都同意 TDD 在某些情境下有价值，我们可能分歧在于「有多少情境」（虽然这很难真正判断）。

第1集里 DHH 攻击「TDD 必然导致重度 mock」，他的处理是**先把术语拆开**（[一手]）：
> "I comment that there are two problems with terminology where different things get conflated: first that DHH's critique of TDD was based on an assumption that you had to use heavy mocking in TDD, which isn't the case; second that there is a difference between self-testing code and TDD."
> 中译：我指出这里有两处术语混淆：第一，DHH 对 TDD 的批评建立在「做 TDD 就必须重度使用 mock」这个假设上，而事实并非如此；第二，「自测试代码」和「TDD」是两回事。

### 2. 用「我不是这个意思的发明人 / 我只是记录者」来降级权威身份

- SE Radio 182（2012，DSL）开场自我介绍（[一手·转写]）：
  > "Well, I'll start off my name's Martin Fowler. I'm a internet loud mouth who speaks a lot incessantly on the topic of software development…"
  > 中译：我先来，我叫 Martin Fowler。我是个互联网大嘴巴，就软件开发这个话题不停地说个没完……
- Refactoring Malapropism（2004，[一手]）：
  > "(Important point, I'm not the father or the inventor of refactoring - just a documenter.)"
  > 中译：（重要的一点：我不是重构之父，也不是重构的发明人——我只是个记录者。）
- 2026 Pragmatic Summit（[一手·转写]）：他开玩笑说自己的职业生涯「mostly about writing down Kent Beck's ideas」（主要是把 Kent Beck 的想法写下来）。

### 3. 被推到「谁对谁错」时，拒绝给出普适答案，把问题转向「情境」

Is TDD Dead 第5集，[一手]：
> "I say that this is exactly how someone should take on TDD (or any technique). Try it out, overuse it, settle into a mode that works for you."
> 中译：我说这正是任何人接触 TDD（或任何技术）应有的态度：去试，用过头，然后稳定到适合你的模式。
> "You need to try it, use it, overuse it, and find what works for you and your team. We're not in a codified science, so we have to work with our own experience."
> 中译：你得去试、去用、用到过头，然后找到适合你和你的团队的做法。我们这门行当不是一门有法典的科学，只能靠自己的经验。

同集他关于「dogma」的自述（[一手]）：
> "I agreed with that dislike for dogmatic statements. I get suspicious if I can't find arguments against something I'm describing."
> 中译：我同意（DHH）对教条式说法的反感。如果我自己找不到反对我正在描述的东西的论据，我就会起疑。

### 4. 面对「重构是不是浪费时间」这类质问：改用经济学论证，不用工匠精神

他反复讲同一套回答框架，见 **Economics of Software Design** 演讲（多处版本，站点视频页）：
> "Often people justify effort on software design by pointing to the need for greater craftsmanship and quality. My view is that such moralizing arguments are wrong, that instead we should focus on the economics."
> 中译：人们常常用「需要更高的工匠精神和质量」来为设计投入辩护。我认为这种道德化的论证是错的，我们应该聚焦经济学。
见 https://martinfowler.com/videos.html#economics-software-design

配合 **Design Stamina Hypothesis**：https://martinfowler.com/bliki/DesignStaminaHypothesis.html

### 5. 面对「重构」被滥用：直接划语义边界，并承认自己在打一场可能输的仗

Refactoring Malapropism（2004，[一手]）：
> "If somebody talks about a system being broken for a couple of days while they are refactoring, you can be pretty sure they are not refactoring."
> 中译：如果有人说「我们正在重构，系统会坏掉两天」，你基本可以确定他做的不是重构。
> "I realize I may be fighting a losing game here, but I do want to preserve the precision of the Definition of Refactoring."
> 中译：我意识到这可能是一场注定要输的仗，但我确实想保住「重构」这个定义的精确性。

2018 Agile Australia 上他更直接（[一手]，他本人编辑的 transcript）：
> "Now it's a horrible thing when somebody tweets something along the lines of: 'I'm refactoring our software at the moment, so it's going to be broken for the next two weeks.' *Bzzz* - that's not refactoring."
> 中译：有人发推说「我正在重构我们的软件，所以接下来两周它会挂掉」——这是很糟糕的事。*哔*——那不叫重构。

### 6. 「microservices 是不是过度设计」：先站到对方杠精的位置，再给条件

Microservice Premium（2015-05-13，[一手]）https://martinfowler.com/bliki/MicroservicePremium.html
> "Enough to get everyone's over-hyped-bullshit detector up and flashing."
> 中译：（会议每个 session 都在谈 microservices，）足够让每个人的「过度炒作胡扯探测器」响起来并闪烁。
> "any decent answer to an interesting question begins, 'it depends...' -- Kent Beck"
> 中译：任何对一个有意思的问题的像样回答，都从「看情况……」开始。——Kent Beck（他引用）
> "So my primary guideline would be **don't even consider microservices unless you have a system that's too complex to manage as a monolith**."
> 中译：所以我的首要准则是：**除非你的系统复杂到单体已经管不动了，否则连考虑都别考虑 microservices。**
> "The majority of software systems should be built as a single monolithic application."
> 中译：大多数软件系统都应该做成单体应用。

### 7. 最难被反驳的一招：**把反对者的文章登在自己站上**

2015-06-09，Stefan Tilkov 的《Don't start with a monolith》就发布在 martinfowler.com 上，明确反对他的 MonolithFirst，并致谢「感谢 Fowler 对本文早期版本的评论，也感谢这几周持续的讨论」。
https://martinfowler.com/articles/dont-start-monolith.html
MonolithFirst 原文里他也主动标出「不是所有人都同意」，并内链到这篇反对文章：
> "While the bulk of my contacts lean toward the monolith-first approach, it is [by no means unanimous](/articles/dont-start-monolith.html)."
> 中译：虽然我接触的人多数倾向 monolith-first，但**绝非一致**。

## 立场变化的瞬间

### 1. Microservices：从「2014 年的风潮推广者」→「2015 年的泼冷水者」→「2020 年对首要卖点表示怀疑」

- **2014**：goto Berlin 演讲《Microservices》，定义、对比单体、列出「部署前必须搞定的事」。当时是「热门的软件架构」。
- **2015-05-13**：《Microservice Premium》。核心转向（[一手]）：
  > "we've seen teams be too eager to embrace microservices, not realizing that microservices introduce complexity on their own account. This adds a premium to a project's cost and risk - one that often gets projects into serious trouble."
  > 中译：我们看到团队过于急切地拥抱 microservices，没意识到 microservices 本身就引入复杂度。这会给项目成本与风险加上一笔溢价，常常把项目拖进大麻烦。
  并引用他自己所在公司的雷达把它命名为 **Microservice Envy**（微服务嫉妒）。
- **2015-06-03**：《Monolith First》（[一手]）

## 关于重构目录与机械化的表述

### 1. 「目录才是这本书的心脏」——第二版的整体结构逻辑

《Changes for the 2nd Edition of Refactoring》（2018-09-05，[一手]）https://martinfowler.com/articles/refactoring-2nd-changes.html
> "After those introductory chapters, I continue with the catalog, which **I've always seen as the heart of the book**."
> 中译：在那些介绍性章节之后，我继续进入目录（catalog）——**我一直把它视为这本书的心脏**。
> "At the broadest level, the second edition's structure follows that of the first edition. That shouldn't be too surprising, since the first edition was so successful, I might as well continue with what worked."
> 中译：在最宏观的层面上，第二版的结构沿用第一版。这不该太让人意外：既然第一版如此成功，我干脆继续用行得通的东西。

**结构 = 四章叙述 + 目录**（[一手]，同上）：
> "The first edition opened with four narrative chapters, these all appear the second edition too."
> 中译：第一版以四个叙述性章节开场，这些在第二版里也都在。
> "I put together an initial chapter of refactorings that contains what I judge to be a good set of refactorings to learn about first."
> 中译：我另加了一章，里面是我判断的一组「应该先学」的重构。

**为什么砍掉「大重构」和尾章**（[一手]）：
> "The big refactorings were always a bit different to the majority of the refactorings, and I do feel these examples work better through essays on my website."
> 中译：那几个「大重构」一直和大多数重构不太一样，我确实觉得这类例子更适合做成网站上的文章。
> "That's the same reason why I also dropped the four 'big refactorings' from the catalog."
> 中译：这也是我把四个「大重构」从目录里拿掉的原因。

### 2. 「叙述书 + 参考书」二合一的体裁原理 —— **Duplex Book**

这是理解「catalog 与原则为什么分两半」的**体裁学解释**，2007-06-13 他专门写了一篇 bliki：
https://martinfowler.com/bliki/DuplexBook.html （[一手]）
> "But xUnit Test Patterns isn't as scary as it looks, because it's actually two books in one set of covers. In this it follows a style I also used in P of EAA. The first book is a narrative book, designed to be read 'cover to cover'. … The second book is reference material, which is designed not to be read cover to cover … but instead to be dipped into when needed."
> 中译：但《xUnit Test Patterns》没有看上去那么吓人，因为它实际上是装在同一副封面里的两本书。它沿用的也是我在《企业应用架构模式》里用过的风格。第一本是叙述书，设计成从头读到尾的；第二本是参考资料，设计成**不要**通读、而是需要时去翻的。
> "My personal rule of thumb is that 200 pages is the limit for a narrative cover-to-cover technical book."
> 中译：我个人的经验法则是：一本能通读的技术书，叙述部分上限是 200 页。
> "The point of all this is that we should consider web articles as information resources, resources that can and should be extended and revised…"
> 中译：（另一篇里）重点是：我们应该把网络文章看作信息资源，可以也应该被扩充和修订。

**萧潇的判断 [推断]**：他把书分成 catalog 与原则，不是分类癖，是**「200 页可通读上限」这一条个人硬规则倒逼出来的结构**。原则部分服务于「读一遍就懂」，catalog 部分服务于「需要时查阅」。工具化（IDE 自动重构）也服务同一目的：让「查阅」这一步尽量不需要人。

### 3. 机械化的态度：**工具是好帮手，但不是必须的**

refactoring.com 首页（[一手]）https://refactoring.com/
> "## Automated tools are helpful, but not essential
> When I wrote the first edition of Refactoring, back in the late 90's, there were few automated tools that supported Refactoring. Now many languages have IDEs which automate many common refactorings. These are a really valuable part of my toolkit allowing me to carry out refactoring faster. But such tools aren't essential - I often work in programming languages without tool support, in which case I rely on taking small steps, and using frequent testing to detect mistakes."
> 中译：**自动化工具很有帮助，但不是必需的。** 我在 90 年代末写第一版时，几乎没有支持重构的自动化工具。现在很多语言有 IDE 能自动完成常见重构。它们是我工具箱里非常有价值的一部分，让我能更快重构。但这类工具不是必需的——我常在那些没有工具支持的语言里工作，那时我依靠「小步走 + 频繁测试来发现错误」。

同页对「重构是不是要专门排期」的立场（[一手]）：
> "Refactoring isn't a special task that would show up in a project plan. Done well, it's a regular part of programming activity."
> 中译：重构不是会在项目计划里出现的一项特殊任务。做得好时，它是编程活动的日常部分。
> "Refactoring isn't another word for cleaning up code - it specifically defines *one* technique for improving the health of a code-base."
> 中译：重构不是「清理代码」的同义词——它具体地定义了改善代码库健康的**一种**技术。

### 4. 机械化的前提：小步 + 行为不变 = 可机械化

refactoring.com 对重构的定义（[一手]）：
> "Its heart is a series of small behavior preserving transformations. Each transformation (called a 'refactoring') does little, but a sequence of these transformations can produce a significant restructuring. Since each refactoring is small, it's less likely to go wrong. The system is kept fully working after each refactoring."
> 中译：它的核心是一系列小的、保持行为的变换。每个变换（称为一个「重构」）做的不多，但一串变换能产生重大的结构重组。因为每个重构都小，出错的概率就更低。每次重构之后系统都保持完全可用。

**这正是可机械化的技术前提**：行为保存（behavior-preserving）→ 可以被工具自动执行 → 可以被 LLM 生成。[推断]

> 中译：它常被放在 TDD 的语境里描述，但开发者的工作流里有很多地方可以用重构。**机会主义式**地使用它尤其重要，这样问题一显现就能被修掉。定期重构的价值不在于某种工匠精神的号召，而在于商业理由——保证未来有价值软件的流动。

### 6. 与「机械化」相关的最新一手材料：AI 时代的重构经济学

- 《The Economic Benefit of Refactoring》（Giles Edwards-Alexander，2026-07-30，他站点发表）：实验「把大函数拆开是否降低 token 成本」，暗示**现在有可能测量重构的经济收益**。https://martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai/refactoring-economic-benefit.html
- Pragmatic Engineer 播客（2025-11-19）要点之一：「why refactoring is more important than ever」「how combining AI tools with deterministic techniques may be what engineering teams need」。
- **Workflows of Refactoring 的 infodeck 版**（他是 infodeck 这种「可翻阅的演示文稿」体裁的作者），本身就是他「把静态文档做成可交互工具」的实践。

We shouldn't let the Print Age notions of how articles should be constructed dictate the patterns of the Internet Age."
> 中译：这一切的重点是：我们应该把网络文章视为信息资源，是会而且应该随着我们理解的加深、时间和精力的允许而被扩充和修订的。我们不该让印刷时代的文章构造观念支配互联网时代的模式。

## 关于 AI 生成代码的近期表态

> 这一节对「全部代码由 AI 生成、单人维护」的用户场景最关键，按时间倒序。

### 1. 核心概念区分：Vibe Coding ≠ Agentic Programming（2026-05-21）

两条 bliki，同一天发布，是他给这场混乱命名的正式表态：

**Vibe Coding** https://martinfowler.com/bliki/VibeCoding.html （[一手]）
> "Vibe coding is building a software application by prompting an LLM, telling it what to build, trying it out, prompting for changes - but without looking at any of the code that the LLM generates."
> 中译：Vibe coding 是通过提示 LLM 来构建软件应用——告诉它要建什么、试用它、提示修改——**但不看 LLM 生成的任何代码**。
> "The key point about vibe coding is 'forget that the code even exists'. This is what gives it much of its usefulness, but also its limitations."
> 中译：vibe coding 的关键点是「忘记代码的存在」。这既给了它大部分用处，也给了它局限。
> "the resulting software often shows problems with maintainability, correctness, and security - so is best used for disposable software written for a limited audience."
> 中译：产出软件常在可维护性、正确性和安全性上有问题，所以最好只用于为有限受众写的、用完即弃的软件。
> "On the whole vibe coding software is best used for disposable software that's only used by its author or a close group of collaborators who understand and accept the risks involved. Code that is more complex, more widely-used, and with more consequences to its risks should not be forgotten about."
> 中译：总的来说，vibe coding 出来的软件最好只用于一次性软件，且只由其作者或一小群理解并接受相关风险的协作者使用。更复杂、使用更广、风险后果更重的代码，不该被「忘记」。

**Agentic Programming** https://martinfowler.com/bliki/AgenticProgramming.html （[一手]）
> "Increasingly software developers are not typing code into their IDEs. Instead they prompt an LLM to do so, then review the results. This is a profound change to the nature of programming, where humans oversee LLM agents who generate the code. **Humans are still responsible for what the software does and how it works**, but use different skills to create their products."
> 中译：越来越多的开发者不再往 IDE 里敲代码，而是提示 LLM 去写，然后审查结果。这是编程本质的深刻变化：人类监督生成代码的 LLM 代理。**人类仍然对软件做什么、如何工作负责**，只是用了不同的技能来创造产品。
> "I like to distinguish agentic programming from Vibe Coding. With vibe coding humans don't look at the code, indeed they forget that it even exists, while with agentic programming they are concerned with the code, often giving it detailed review."
> 中译：我喜欢把 agentic programming 和 vibe coding 区分开。vibe coding 里人不看代码，甚至忘了它存在；agentic programming 里人在意代码，常常做细致审查。
> "At this point harness engineering, focusing on working on the guides and sensors around the LLM seem central. Additionally this raises the importance of programmers understanding the domain they are working with."
> 中译：在目前这个阶段，**harness engineering**——专注于打磨 LLM 周围的「导轨（guides）与传感器（sensors）」——似乎是核心。此外这也提升了程序员理解自己所在领域的重要性。

**保留矛盾**：他在 Vibe Coding 条目里遗憾地承认：
> "Sadly the term 'vibe coding' really caught on, so many people use it to mean agentic programming."
> 中译：遗憾的是「vibe coding」这个词真的流行起来了，很多人用它来指 agentic programming。
——这是他 2006 年 Semantic Diffusion 那套诊断在二十年后的重演，他本人是这么看的。[推断，但文中明确使用了他自己造的 Semantic Diffusion 概念并链接过去]

### 2. 最贴近用户场景的一篇：单人 / 小规模 AI 编程的边界（2025-08-28）

《Some thoughts on LLMs and Software Development》https://martinfowler.com/articles/202508-ai-thoughts.html （[一手]）

**（a）他怀疑现有生产率调查，因为不分工作流：**
> "One of the big problems with these surveys is that they aren't taking into account *how* people are using the LLMs. From what I can tell the vast majority of LLM usage is fancy auto-complete, often using co-pilot. But those I know who get the most value from LLMs reckon that auto-complete isn't very useful, preferring approaches that allow the LLM to directly read and edit source code files to carry out tasks."
> 中译：这些调查的一大问题是没考虑人们**怎么**用 LLM。据我所见，绝大多数用量是「高级自动补全」，常常是 Copilot。但我认识的那些从 LLM 获益最多的人认为自动补全不太有用，他们更喜欢让 LLM 直接读写源码文件来完成任务的方式。

**（b）关于「LLM 说测试全绿但其实有失败」——直接对应用户场景：**
> "I've often heard, with decent reason, an LLM compared to a junior colleague. But I find LLMs are quite happy to say 'all tests green', yet when I run them, there are failures. If that was a junior engineer's behavior, how long would it be before H.R. was involved?"
> 中译：我常听到（有相当道理地）把 LLM 比作初级同事。但我发现 LLM 很乐意说「测试全绿」，而我自己一跑，有失败。如果这是初级工程师的行为，多久 HR 就要介入了？

**（c）幻觉是特性，不是 bug：**
> "My former colleague Rebecca Parsons, has been saying for a long time that hallucinations aren't a bug of LLMs, they are a feature. Indeed they are *the* feature. All an LLM does is produce hallucinations, it's just that we find some of them useful."
> 中译：我的前同事 Rebecca Parsons 长期以来一直说，幻觉不是 LLM 的 bug，而是 feature。它们确实是**那个** feature。LLM 做的全部事情就是产生幻觉，只不过我们发现其中一些有用。
> "Certainly if we ever ask a hallucination engine for a numeric answer, we should ask it at least three times, so we get some sense of the variation. Furthermore we shouldn't ask an LLM to calculate an answer than we can calculate deterministically (yes, I've seen this)."
> 中译：如果我们真要从一台「幻觉引擎」那里要数值答案，至少该问三次，好对波动有点感觉。而且我们不该让 LLM 去算一个我们能用确定性方法算出来的答案（是的，我见过这种事）。

**（d）非确定性 = 工程学上的容差问题：**
> "Other forms of engineering have to take into account the variability of the world. A structural engineer builds in tolerance for all the factors she can't measure. … Software Engineering is unusual in that it works with deterministic machines. Maybe LLMs mark the point where we join our engineering peers in a world of non-determinism."
> 中译：其他形式的工程必须考虑世界的多变性。结构工程师会为她无法测量的所有因素留出容差。……软件工程的特殊之处在于它面对的是确定性机器。也许 LLM 标志着我们加入工程同行、进入一个非确定性世界的那个点。

**（e）安全：Lethal Trifecta**
> "LLMs create a huge increase in the attack surface of software systems. Simon Willison described the The Lethal Trifecta for AI agents: an agent that combines access to your private data, exposure to untrusted content, and a way to externally communicate ('exfiltration')."
> 中译：LLM 让软件系统的攻击面大幅增加。Simon Willison 描述了 AI agent 的「致命三件套」：同时具备访问你的私有数据、暴露于不可信内容、以及对外通信（外泄）的能力。

### 3. 播客里最完整的一段：容差与「我们会在安全上撞车」（2025-11-19）

Pragmatic Engineer 播客，Gergely 提问「这是第一次我们见到一个对软件工程而言如此广泛的非确定性工具吗？」（[一手·转写]）
> Martin: "It's a whole new way of thinking. It's got some interesting parallels to other forms of engineering. **In other forms of engineering, you think in terms of tolerances.** My wife's a structural engineer. She always thinks in terms of what are the tolerances… Because I mostly know what the properties of wood or concrete or steel are, but I've got to go for the worst case. We need probably some of that kind of thinking ourselves. **What are the tolerances of the non-determinism that we have to deal with?** We need to realize that we can't skate too close to the edge because otherwise we're going to have some bridges collapsing. And I suspect we're going to do that, particularly on the security side. We're going to have some noticeable crashes. I fear because people have got skated way too close to the edge in terms of the non-determinism of the tools they're using."
> 中译：这是一整套新的思维方式。它和别的工程形式有一些有趣的相似之处。**在别的工程领域，你是用「容差」来思考的。** 我妻子是结构工程师。她总是想的是容差是多少……因为木材、混凝土或钢材的性能我大体知道，但我必须按最坏情况来。我们大概也需要一些这类思维。**我们必须面对的非确定性，它的容差是多少？** 我们必须认识到不能贴着边缘滑行，否则就会有大桥垮塌。我怀疑我们会这么干，尤其是在安全方面。我们会看到一些显眼的崩塌。我担心，因为人们在所用工具的非确定性上贴边缘贴得太近了。

同集另一段（谈大公司是「复杂的烂摊子」）：
> "Companies are complicated — and often not logical because they are built by humans, not by computers. And there's all sorts of history in there… And all of this stuff builds up over time to a complicated mess. And any big company is going to have that kind of complicated mess. It's very hard to not get that situation."
> 中译：公司是复杂的——而且常常不合逻辑，因为它们是人建的，不是计算机建的。里面沉积着各种各样的历史……所有这些东西随时间堆成一个复杂的烂摊子。任何大公司都会有这种复杂的烂摊子。很难不落到那个局面。

### 4. 「AI 是放大器 / snake oil 会重演」——Pragmatic Summit 2026-04-07

（[一手·转写]，Gergely Orosz 整理发布）
> "I suspect there will still be some similarities with Agile. The core notions behind Agile and extreme programming are solid and good, but a huge snake-oil industry appeared around it – the 'Agile industrial complex', as I refer to it. This is also happening with AI right now, and it's often hard to see the difference between snake oil and the real stuff."
> 中译：我猜（AI）和 Agile 还是会有相似之处。Agile 和极限编程背后的核心观念是扎实的、好的，但它周围出现了一个巨大的 snake oil（假药）产业——我称之为「敏捷工业复合体」。AI 眼下也在发生同样的事，而且常常很难分辨假药和真货。

关于「一披萨团队 vs 两披萨团队」（他倾向保留人的协作）：
> "Are we seeing two-pizza teams becoming one-pizza teams because agents don't eat pizza, or do we see two-pizza teams staying and becoming much more effective and capable? **My bet is on more effective two-pizza teams.** … I don't necessarily think that one person and many genies is the right answer."
> 中译：我们会看到两披萨团队因为 agent 不吃披萨而变成一披萨团队，还是两披萨团队保留下来、变得更强更能干？**我押后者。**……我不认为「一个人 + 很多精灵」必然是正确答案。

关于「负面价值」与倦怠（Gergely 要点第 4 条，[一手·转写]）：
> "Martin suggests to catch when you start producing 'negative value': that's when to take a break."
> 中译：Martin 建议：当你开始产出「负价值」时，就该停下来休息。

### 5. 他对 LLM 的「人格化」清单（2025-07-22）——极好用的表达 DNA

《Who is LLM?》https://martinfowler.com/articles/who-is-llm.html （[一手]）
他收集并评价了四种给 LLM 起人格的做法：
- **倔驴 Dusty**（同事 Birgitta 的比喻）：eager to help / stubborn / very well-read but inexperienced / won't admit when it doesn't "know" something
- **神灯精灵 Genie**（Kent Beck）："the genie does what you tell it to do. But perhaps not quite in the way you would have wished."
- **老虎机 slot machine**（Kent Beck）："Sometimes you get a fantastic result, often you get dreck, and the distribution between the two seems random."
- **Uriah Heep**（他自己提的，狄更斯《大卫·科波菲尔》里的谄媚小人）：
  > "Heep is infuriatingly obsequious, constantly stressing how 'umble' he is and how he serves only to do his betters' bidding. But he is full of malice: manipulating, and essentially controlling those people he 'serves'."
  > 中译：Heep 谄媚得令人恼火，不断强调自己多么「卑微」、只服务于主子的吩咐。但他满怀恶意：操纵、并且实质上控制着那些他「服务」的人。

并且他点出关键机制：
> "a common story is that we have to invest significant effort to learn how to use them well. Hitting the jackpot makes them seem like they are easy to use, but that's a mirage, which can skew our expectations of the technology, leading to frustration."
> 中译：一个共同的故事是：我们必须投入可观的努力才能学会用好它们。**中一次大奖会让它们看起来很容易用，但那是个海市蜃楼**，会扭曲我们对这项技术的预期，进而带来挫败。

### 6. 2026 年的最新状态：harness、自托管、以及「不能外包验收标准」

**Fragments 2026-07-13**（瑞士 Engelberg「软件开发的未来」闭门会笔记，[一手]）https://martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-07-13.html
> "Will our attention on harnesses last long enough for our next retreat? Will the models just get so good that harnesses become unnecessary? Those with some mechanical sympathy for LLMs seem to think not - but are they overly coupled to the current state of technology? I find such speculation tends not to lead anywhere useful."
> 中译：我们对 harness 的注意力能撑到下次闭门会吗？模型会不会好到让 harness 变得不必要？对 LLM 有些「机械同理心」的人似乎认为不会——但他们是不是过度绑定于当前技术状态？我觉得这种推测通常通向不了有用的地方。
> 有一段关于 context 的实操细节：「一位参加者把 `agents.md` 控制在 200 行以内。」
> "We can outsource many things, but not the acceptance criteria, at some point there's a human request and a human judgment on whether that request was properly executed."
> 中译：我们可以外包很多事，但**不能外包验收标准**；在某个点上，总有一个人的请求，和一个关于这个请求是否被正确执行的人的判断。
> "My sense is that model building is still important, and while I agree that the genie can take an active role in that construction, I don't think the human can entirely outsource it. Even if the genie builds the model itself, it needs to teach us that model, because the model helps us imagine and communicate the goals."
> 中译：我的感觉是，构建模型仍然重要；我同意精灵可以在这个过程中扮演积极角色，但我不认为人可以完全把它外包出去。即使精灵自己建好了模型，它也需要把那个模型教给我们，因为模型帮助我们想象和沟通目标。

关于「agent 会插入没人要求的功能」：
> "One of the perils of agent-developed code is their habit of inserting features that were never asked for. One team spent three days trying to figure out such an unrequested feature, trying to figure out who had requested it and if anyone wanted to keep it."
> 中译：agent 开发代码的风险之一是它们习惯插入从未被要求的功能。有个团队花了三天去搞清这么一个没人要的功能，想弄明白是谁提的需求、以及有没有人想留着它。

**Fragments 2026-09-08**（[一手]）https://martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-09-08.html
> "The danger is that people use lots AI automation while using incomplete measurements of its effectiveness, leading to short-term dashboards going up, but disaster in longer time-scales. He refers to these illusory short-term gains as **counterfeit utility**."
> 中译：危险在于人们大量使用 AI 自动化，同时用不完整的指标衡量其有效性——短期看板往上走，更长时间尺度上是灾难。他把这种虚幻的短期收益称为「伪币式的效用（counterfeit utility）」。

> 中译：要有效应对 AI，我们需要改变激励，让人们**在验证上的投入超过在生成上的投入**。否则我们开的是一辆引擎强劲、刹车孱弱的车。

  > 中译：在我埋头把它做出来的时候，agent 们强了很多。等我抬起头来，我的 harness 已经不需要了。事实上，除了最宽松的 harness 之外，任何 harness 可能都不需要了。

### 7. 关于「AI 写文章」的强烈负面表态（2026-09-01，对 Skill 写作同样适用）

Fragments 2026-09-01（[一手]）https://martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-09-01.html
> "A while ago, I was thinking that it was reasonable for folks who aren't as committed to writing as I am to use an AI to help polish their prose. Now I'm turning to encouraging writers to reject it. That pervasive LLM-voice is just so common now, my sense is that it discredits the writing even before the reader has a chance to try to understand what is being said."
> 中译：不久前我还认为，对那些不像我这样投入写作的人来说，用 AI 打磨文字是合理的。现在我转为劝写作者拒绝它。那种弥漫的「LLM 腔」现在太普遍了，我的感觉是：**在读者有机会去理解你在说什么之前，它就已经让这篇文章失去了可信度。**
> "I'm not sure writers can edit the LLM-ness out of prose once it's in there."
> 中译：我不确定，一旦 LLM 味进了文章里，作者还能不能把它编辑掉。

  > "Once you've got a reasonable draft, read it out loud. By doing this you'll find bits that don't sound right, and need to fix."
  > 中译：等你有了一个像样的草稿，把它**读出声来**。这样做你会找到读起来不对、需要改的地方。

### 9. 与「单人维护全部 AI 生成代码」场景最相关的推论链 [推断]

3. **真正的关键动作是 harness engineering**：打磨 LLM 周围的**导轨（guides：上下文、架构约束、agents.md）与传感器（sensors：测试、类型、不变量、安全扫描）**，让 agent 能自纠（AgenticProgramming + Harness engineering 文章 + 2026-07-13 fragments）。

5. **有一件事不能外包：验收标准**（2026-07-13 fragments）。
