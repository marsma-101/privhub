## 3. 修订版序言（Preface）——一手长文

URL: https://jnd.org/preface-design-of-everyday-things-revised-edition/
发布日期：2013 年 4 月 22 日

原文关键句：

- "This is a starter kit for good design."
- "One goal is to turn everyone into great observers of the absurd, of the poor design that gives rise to so many of the problems of modern life, especially of modern technology."
- "Good design is actually a lot harder to notice than poor design, in part because good designs fit our needs so well that the design is invisible, serving us without drawing attention to itself. Bad design, on the other hand, screams out its inadequacies, making itself very noticeable."
- "everyone is a designer in the sense that all of us deliberately design our lives, our rooms, and the way we do things."
- "**Signifiers** are the most important addition to the chapter, a concept first introduced in my book *Living with Complexity*. The first edition had a focus upon affordances, but although affordances make sense for interaction with physical objects, they are confusing when dealing with virtual ones. As a result, affordances have created much confusion in the world of design. Affordances define what actions are possible. Signifiers specify how people discover those possibilities: signifiers are signs, perceptible signals of what can be done. **Signifiers are of far more importance to designers than are affordances.**"
- 术语归属："user experience" —— "a term that I was among the first to use, when in the early 1990s, the group I headed at Apple called itself 'the User Experience Architect's office'."
- 关于错误的分类修订："I now divide slips into two main categories – action based and memory lapses – and mistakes into three categories – rule based, knowledge based, and memory lapses."
- 关于设计思考那一章："The chapter then takes a radical shift in position, starting with a section entitled 'What I just told you? It doesn't really work that way.' Here is where I introduce **Norman's law: The day the product team is announced, it is behind schedule and over its budget.**"
- 关于活动中心设计："Then I introduce activity-centered design as a more appropriate variant of human-centered design in many circumstances."
- 关于增量与激进创新："The techniques of human-centered design are appropriate to incremental innovation: they cannot lead to radical innovations."
- 关于心理学不变而技术变："With the passage of time, the psychology of people stays the same, but the tools and objects in the world change. Cultures change. Technologies change. The principles of design still hold, but the way they get applied needs to be modified to account for new activities, new technologies, new methods of communication and interaction."
- 关于商业现实："To understand products, it is not enough to understand design or technology: it is critical to understand business."
- "**The best products do not always succeed.** Brilliant new technologies might take decades to become accepted."
- 关于删减："I had to include my learnings from these experiences." / "I tried to delete as much as I added to keep the total size about the same."

## 5. 《Human-Centered Design Considered Harmful》全文要点

URL: https://jnd.org/human-centered-design-considered-harmful/
原始出处：*Interactions*, 12(4), July+August 2005, pp. 14–19（ACM）。jnd.org 页标注 November 17, 2008（作者版）。
后续澄清文：https://jnd.org/hcd_harmful_a_clarification/ （"HCD harmful? A Clarification"）

核心论点（原文）：
- "Human-Centered Design has become such a dominant theme in design that it is now accepted by interface and application designers automatically, without thought, let alone criticism. That's a dangerous state — when things are treated as accepted wisdom."
- "**Activity-Centered Design is superior.**"
- 活动层级（原文）："At the highest levels are *activities*, which are comprised of *tasks*, which themselves are comprised of *actions*, and actions are made up of *operations*." 理论来源他自称 "my own brand of 'Activity Theory,' heavily motivated by early Russian and Scandinavian research." 定义："an activity is a coordinated, integrated set of tasks."
- **静态界面 vs 动态序列**（对密集界面设计极关键）："The methods of HCD seem centered around static understanding of each set of controls, each screen on an electronic display. But as a result, the sequential operations of activities are often ill-supported."
- 引 Christine Frederick《The Labor-Saving Kitchen》(1919)："We find that work in the kitchen does not consist of independent, separate acts, but of a series of inter-related processes." 诺曼评论："Simply delete the phrase 'in the kitchen' and her words are still a powerful prescription for design."
- **工具与活动的关系**："People do adapt to technology." / "Learn the activity, and the tools are understood. That's the mantra of the Human-Centered Design community. But this is actually a misleading statement, because for many activities, **the tools define the activity**. Maybe the reality is just the converse: **Learn the tools, and the activity is understood.**"
- "To the Human-Centered Design community, the tool should be invisible, it should not get in the way. **With Activity-Centered Design, the tool is the way.**"
- **听用户的两难**："listening to customers is always wise, but acceding to their requests can lead to overly complex designs." / "If a user suggestion fails to fit within this design model, it should be discarded." / "**Paradoxically, the best way to satisfy users is sometimes to ignore them.**"
- "Sometimes what is needed is a **design dictator** who says, 'Ignore what users say: I know what's best for them.'"
- 对 personas 的质疑："did knowing that the persona is that of a 37 year old, single mother, studying for the MBA at night, really help lay out the control panel or determine the screen layout and, more importantly, to design the appropriate action sequence?"
- 对 HCD 能力的质疑："Human-Centered Design does **not** guarantee good products. It can lead to clear improvements of bad ones. Moreover, good Human-Centered Design will avoid failures. It will ensure that products do work, that people can use them. But is good design the goal? Many of us wish for great design. Great design, I contend, comes from breaking the rules, by ignoring the generally accepted practices, by pushing forward with a clear concept of the end result, no matter what."
- 自我限定（重要，体现他的怀疑主义）："My precepts here are themselves dangerous. We dare not let the entire world of designers follow their instincts and ignore conventional wisdom: most lack the deep understanding of the activity coupled with a clear conceptual model."

## 5b. 《HCD harmful? A Clarification》——作者本人的自我澄清（**对密集界面设计最关键的一篇**）

URL: https://jnd.org/hcd-harmful-a-clarification/
页内标注：November 17, 2008

原文关键句：
- 承认争议规模（原文括注）："*(Hah, that's an understatement! There must be 500 comments and blogs posted about it.)*"
- 否认自己转向："I do not think this to be any change in what I have advocated. Rather, I see all my work as part of a coherent pattern, moving toward products and services that truly fit human needs."
- **HCD 的病理**："HCD has developed as a limited view of design. Instead of looking at a person's entire activity, it has primarily focused upon **page-by-page analysis, screen-by-screen**. As a result, **sequences, interruptions, ill-defined goals** — all the aspects of real activities, have been ignored."
- **关于错误信息（金句，极重要）**："And error messages — **there should not be any error messages. All messages should contain explanations and offer alternative ways of proceeding from the message itself.**"
- 对 personas 的定性："There has been far too much emphasis on individual people, trying to model them, trying to build fascinating scenarios and 'personas.' I think much of this work misplaced, irrelevant, and potentially harmful if it diverts the limited time and resources of the design team away from matters that can actually help."
- "Are Scenarios and Personas worthless? No, scenarios are great for marketing. Personas are great for communication... **But for great design, design for the activity.**"
- **任务与活动的定义（原文）**："**Tasks** are situations with a single, well-specified goal, such as 'respond to this email.' **Activities** are larger groupings, comprised of multiple tasks that fit together, such as 'get caught up on the day's correspondence' which means reading email, responding, looking up information, sometimes to copy and paste into emails, checking calendars, and other associated, related tasks."
- "To me, **error analysis is the sweet spot for improvement.**"
- **设计重心（对错误路径的设计）**："It doesn't matter whether the analysis is called task analysis, task-flow diagram, scenario, activity analysis, or activity-flow diagram. What does matter is that it be **a detailed analysis of the steps a person might follow when things go wrong**. What should you tell the person? What choices should you offer? What would the person wish to do in each of the situations?"
- **完美路径容易，异常路径难（对密集界面极关键）**："It is relatively easy to design for the perfect cases, when everything goes right, or when all the information required is available in proper format. Good design, however, will **handle the unexpected situation**, when there are special cases, when the information is entered incorrectly or incompletely, or correctly, but in the wrong location or wrong sequence. **This is where the difference arises between a pleasant experience and a frustrating one.**"
- **"帮助" vs "协助"（原文区分）**：把错误信息改造成协助 —— "Not 'help' which tells the person what should have been done, but '**assistance**' which offers the proper action and makes it so easy to proceed that the person might deliberately type incomplete information to get the guidance."
- "Remember: the 'perfect' behavior seldom arises. Almost every situation is a special case of one sort or another. So **design for the special cases, design to eliminate error messages.**"
- 收尾："**Design for the activity and the rest will take care of itself**, better than the reverse — design for the person, without proper support for the activity."
- 相关文章：[Ad-Hoc Personas & Empathetic Focus](https://jnd.org/ad-hoc_personas_empathetic_focus/)

## 6c. 《How I taught DBW》（2025-06-04，最近一次确认的 jnd.org 长文）

URL: https://jnd.org/how-i-taught-dbw/

- 页面标题实为 "Teaching Design for a Better World: Lessons from My Class"，发布于 **2025 年 6 月 4 日**（配图目录含 `/2025/09/` 路径，说明该页 2025 年 9 月仍在更新）。
- 书稿最初名为《**Four Maxims to Save the World**》，他自述"It wasn't working"，于是在第二轮课程中彻底重构为《Design for a Better World》。**这是一次明确的自我否定与重构记录。**
- 他自述教学法："As I write my books, I almost always give talks and teach using the material."
- 原始四条箴言（原文）：
  - **Maxim 1**: Measure the Things People Care About and Understand–Quality of Life, not Economics.
  - **Maxim 2**: Move from the Age of Waste to Societal Resilience and Sustainability.
  - **Maxim 3**: Many Things Must Change, Especially the Beliefs and Behaviors that Disrupt Societal, Health, and Ecological Systems.
  - **Maxim 4**: Changes Must Be Collaborative, Designed **With** and **By** the Communities.
- 教学细节（体现他的方法，非空谈）：学生先读材料→上课用 Google Form 出 4 道题、限时 5 分钟、至少答 1 题→**他按二元（0/1）评分，只要证明读过即可，答错也能得 1 分**（"Their answers could be wrong, but even wrong answers could indicate that the student had read the material and was trying to make sense of it."）→ 随后整节课围绕学生答案展开讨论。他评："I highly recommend this method, although it only works with small class sizes."
- 网站 DBW.jnd.org 已于 2025 年下架（"too much work to maintain it"），内容归入 https://jnd.org/category/essays/dbw
- 提到 UCSD Design Lab 牵头、历时 7 年，为圣迭戈-蒂华纳（SD-TJ）赢得 **World Design Capital 2024** 称号。
- 他书中的自我批评（出自其课程讨论题）："The book states that the way we teach design is wrong, **even the course I developed**. Why did I say that?"
- 人体工学式提问示范（可作他"设计要问对问题"的例证）："Which is better: plastic bags or paper? Explain your reasoning. **(If you think the answer is obvious, ask yourself why I would ask.)**"
