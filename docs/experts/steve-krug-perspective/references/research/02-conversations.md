# Steve Krug 长对话 / 播客 / 深度访谈 / 演讲 调研

> 调研范围：即兴思考与口语表达 —— 长访谈、播客、演讲中的原话、比喻、被追问时的反应、立场变化、回避的话题。
> 信源分级：`[一手]` = 访谈/播客原文或官方文字稿（含本人站点引用其本人原话）；`[二手]` = 他人转述、节目简介、摘要；`[推断]` = 调研者推断。
> 黑名单已遵守：未使用知乎、微信公众号、百度百科/百度知道。
> 调研日期：2026 年（Krug 现年 77 岁，住 Brookline / Chestnut Hill, MA）。

## 0. 开场：一句话抓住他的口语人格

三条来自他本人站点、由他本人挑选来代表自己的话，最能定义他的口语表达方式——**自嘲 + 反向坦白 + 电影/电视类比**：

| 原话 | 主题 | 出处 |
|---|---|---|
| "I was the one kid in Catholic school who never wanted to be a priest." | 自传式自嘲 | [sensible.com 首页](https://sensible.com/) `[一手]`（本人引用其本人播客原话） |
| "Writing is like the torture machine in *The Princess Bride* that sucks a year out of your life every few seconds." | 写作的痛苦（核心比喻） | [sensible.com 首页](https://sensible.com/) `[一手]`（同上，出自 Lou Rosenfeld 对谈） |
| "I like to characterize it as a relatively big fish in a very small pond." | 对自己名望的定位 | [Brave UX Ep.015](https://thespaceinbetween.co.nz/brave-ux-podcast/015-steve-krug) `[一手]` |

他在播客里有一套**自认的行为规矩**，且会自己把这条规矩说出来——这是理解他所有"惊人发言"的钥匙：

> "My principle of podcasts is that I always make at least two or three disclosures against interest, where I say something I shouldn't say about myself. One is we should have a little number up in the corner."
> —— [Marketing Speak Ep.349, 2022-06-29](https://www.marketingspeak.com/web-usability-essentials-with-steve-krug/) `[一手]`
>
> （大意：我在播客里有个原则，每次至少说两三句"对自己不利的坦白"。应该给我头上挂个计数器。）

而 Brave UX 那一期的"不该说的话"，他自己在官网点名了位置：

> "This time the thing I shouldn't have said was my answer (at 1:07) to his question 'What do you see as the greatest usability challenge we are facing as a society?'"
> —— [sensible.com 首页](https://sensible.com/) `[一手]`

## 2. 被追问时的回答方式（核心章节）

### 2.1 「3 个用户不够科学 / 不具统计显著性」

这是最能看出他反应模式的例子，而且他自己在《Rocket Surgery Made Easy》每章末的 FAQ 里**预演过这场对话**。Brave UX 里他复述了这段 FAQ：

> "…one of them was basically **you're only testing three people, so it's not statistically significant. And my answer was, if somebody asked you that, somebody's gonna ask you and say, well, it's not [statistically significant]. And your answer should be, 'yep, absolutely. We're testing three, no statistical, no point in even gathering statistics. But the fact is people have been doing this for years and it works. It gets you the insights that you need.'** … and sort of the same thing with representative users is **it just works. I mean, come and watch some and you'll see that it works.**"
> —— [Brave UX Ep.015](https://thespaceinbetween.co.nz/brave-ux-podcast/015-steve-krug) `[一手]`

**拆解他的应答结构（可复用）：**
1. **先全盘认输，不辩解** —— "Yep, absolutely."（对，完全正确）
2. **把对方的科学框架直接作废** —— "no point in even gathering statistics"（这里根本没有统计可言，别拿统计当尺子）
3. **换成实用主义证据** —— "people have been doing this for years and it works"
4. **把举证责任推回去** —— "come and watch some and you'll see that it works"（你来看一场就知道了）
5. **升级到价值观** —— 宁可要一个做了的粗糙测试，也不要一个完美的没做的测试

同一逻辑在"找不到合适被试"那题里，他的第一句话是：

> "**They should lower their standards.** … because nothing should keep them from doing the testing. **Recruiting is hard. Recruiting is the hardest part.** … the more finicky you are about making sure that your participants are a perfect map onto your intended actual users, the harder recruiting tends to be. **And it's the reason why people don't do more testing.**"
> —— [Brave UX Ep.015](https://thespaceinbetween.co.nz/brave-ux-podcast/015-steve-krug) `[一手]`

以及书里的对应格言：

> "**Recruit loosely and grade on a curve**" —— 招募从宽，评分从宽
> —— [Built In, 2020-04-07](https://builtin.com/design-ux/simplicity-ux-steve-krug-interview) `[一手]`

> "**Whether you do the testing or not is far more important than whether you have the right participants. You actually tend to learn valuable lessons from the wrong participants.**"
> —— [Built In, 2020-04-07](https://builtin.com/design-ux/simplicity-ux-steve-krug-interview) `[一手]`（此句亦被 Krug 本人在官网精选引用）

### 2.2 「Don't Make Me Think 太简单 / 过时了 / 会让人变傻」

**关键事实：他并不回避，而是承认对方的批评然后划界。** 他主动提到近年有人质疑书名前提：

> "**I've seen a handful of people writing articles in the last couple of years expressing some skepticism regarding the premise, suggesting there are sites where you want to make people think. And I'm like: 'Right, of course. We all want that.'**"
> —— [Built In, 2020-04-07](https://builtin.com/design-ux/simplicity-ux-steve-krug-interview) `[一手]`

然后他立刻给出书里本来就有、但被标题遮蔽的区分——**这是他应对"太简单"指控的标准解**：

> "'Don't Make Me Think' really means **don't make me think _about things I don't need to think about_**. … You do want them to think about the **meaning of your content**. You want them to think about **_what's in it for them_**. … What you **don't** want them to think about is: 'What is that thing on the screen? What is it for? What's going to happen if I click that?'"
> —— [Built In, 2020-04-07](https://builtin.com/design-ux/simplicity-ux-steve-krug-interview) `[一手]`

**关于"太基础"**（FAQ 里的正式回答）：

> "**I've been doing Web design (or development, marketing, etc.) for a long time. Won't this be too basic for me?**"
> 他的回答：引用一位 Amazon 读者——"If you're new to web design, you'll learn TONS; if you're a seasoned pro, you'll get a solid refresher and maybe even pick up on a few new things." 并补一句：
> "A lot of UX professionals tell me they leaf through it again every time they start a new project, just to remind themselves of all the 'common sense' that they tend to forget."
> —— [sensible.com FAQ 汇总](https://sensible.com/all-the-faq-lists-from-my-site/) `[一手]`

**关于"没有绝对真理"这条被引用的立场**，被 Brendan 直接引他原话追问时，他给的解释：

> "Well, I like the comic that I had in the book where people are trying to decide whether they should use pulldowns, the dropdowns in the interface. And it's like what they want is an answer that that dropdowns are good or dropdowns are bad. And the fact is, **if you've been involved in any serious design process, it's all contextual.** … So there are very few hard, fast rules other than the kind of high level generic things like you need to make it as clear as possible. … **people always want hard and fast rules. They want answers and there aren't that many answers.**"
> —— [Brave UX Ep.015](https://thespaceinbetween.co.nz/brave-ux-podcast/015-steve-krug) `[一手]`

### 2.3 「说了半天，我们就是改不了」——他改推"不完美的事先做一件事"

> "You've seen people get sucked into redesigns when yes, the redesign would be nice, but you're not gonna end up doing it. But on the other hand, **there is a tweak you could make.** … wouldn't fix them, wouldn't be perfect, but would that improve the situation that you have?"
> 格言原话被 Brendan 复述为：**"when fixing problems always do the least that you can do"**，Krug 答："Do. Yeah."（对。）
> —— [Brave UX Ep.015](https://thespaceinbetween.co.nz/brave-ux-podcast/015-steve-krug) `[一手]`

### 2.4 「怎么说服不买账的 stakeholder」——他的招牌答案："骗进来"

> "**My favorite recommendation was always, if you're starting out introducing it to the team, then do a test of your competitors** because everybody's interested in their competitors. And also **you're not gonna make anybody look bad in house because you're not testing your stuff, you're testing their stuff.**"
> "…that's always been my main recommendation for basically **tricking people into getting a taste for the process**. … **Great snacks, I recommend spend as much money as you can on snacks and lunch and whatever and just make it easy.**"
> —— [Brave UX Ep.015](https://thespaceinbetween.co.nz/brave-ux-podcast/015-steve-krug) `[一手]`

**紧接着的一句是他的诚实底色**——他不假装有万能药：

> Brendan："Some people, doesn't matter what they see, they still won't believe."
> Krug："**I think it's very hard to change people's minds about anything.** … **I think people change their minds very rarely. Very rarely.**"
> —— [Brave UX Ep.015](https://thespaceinbetween.co.nz/brave-ux-podcast/015-steve-krug) `[一手]`

### 2.5 「Uber 那种公司你也夸？」——他区分产品与公司

> "I also admire Uber. I think Uber works really well and, **as much as I dislike Uber as a company, the interface is great.** It does exactly what you need."
> —— [Built In, 2020-04-07](https://builtin.com/design-ux/simplicity-ux-steve-krug-interview) `[一手]`

他还主动揭穿自己夸的那个细节可能是假的（罕见的自我拆台）：

> "When you enter where you're going, it shows you cars in your area, which, **I think isn't even true. I suspect that's a fake. But that's what you want.**"
> —— 同上 `[一手]`

### 2.6 被问到"你天天看世界的毛病，是不是很痛苦？"——他拒绝那个前提

这个反应很典型：**主持人预设了一个"专家的诅咒"，他先笑着纠偏，再给一个更温和的真相。**

> Krug："**No, not quite where I thought you were headed.** … I don't think I run into enough problems without looking for trouble. And I feel like they're the same problems everybody else runs into."
> "What I suspected you might be about to ask was doesn't it make me crazy? … **The usability problems are frustrating, but no more than for everybody else. And maybe I'm even a little more tolerant because I kind of know how hard it is to get it right.**"
> —— [Brave UX Ep.015](https://thespaceinbetween.co.nz/brave-ux-podcast/015-steve-krug) `[一手]`

### 2.7 追问下的"软化与限定"模板

观察到的固定句式（全部出自 Brave UX 同一期 `[一手]`）：
- 先扣掉自己权威：**"I'm probably overemphasize this, I'm probably biased"**（谈语音界面可用性时）
- 谈大趋势时先自嘲：**"maybe I'm just optimistic about human nature"**
- 谈不熟的事直接弃权：**"I don't know if there's even anything smart to say about that."**
- 拿不准是共识还是己见时把不确定说出来：**"I haven't nailed it down yet… which often leads me to suspect that it's just me or I'm right. And it's just me. It's hard to tell the difference until you put the hours in."**（Marketing Speak `[一手]`）

## 4. 立场改变 / 承认自己说错的瞬间

### 4.1 Brave UX 1:07 —— 他自己点名的"不该说的话"（已找到具体内容）

任务要求找到"他到底说了什么"。**答案：他说的是「社交媒体彻头彻尾是坏事」。**

问题由 Brendan 提出："**What do you see as the greatest usability challenge that we are facing as a society?**"

> Krug："Good question. The usability challenge. See, the problem is you think about a challenge. **I come right back to I definitely shouldn't say this. I always say something against my own interest in any [interview].**"
> "But **I feel like social media has been nothing but bad.** … yes, there are upsides … **I feel like the net net is minus a thousand percent.** I feel like it's caused more bad effect in society than almost anything in many years. And I mean, I'm biased because I live in the United States and **we practically lost our country because of social media.** … **there couldn't have been [January 6] without social media.**"
> —— [Brave UX Ep.015](https://thespaceinbetween.co.nz/brave-ux-podcast/015-steve-krug) `[一手]`

随后他自己意识到**这没回答"可用性问题"**，于是切换到新冠疫苗预约系统的可用性灾难：

> "it doesn't answer your usability question in terms of usability. … **some of it was at the QA level, but I'm increasingly thinking QA problems are usability problems. If something's broken, then it's not usable.**"
> 具体举例：找到可预约时段 → 填完所有注册信息 → 点提交 → 时段没了 → 下一次要**重新填一遍**所有信息。"**This is not rocket surgery, that those systems should have been storing that information.**"
> 并点出受害人群："a lot of people were trying to do it were over 75 or their relatives were trying to do it remotely for their relatives who are over 75."
> —— 同上 `[一手]`

**两层意义：**
1. 表层"不该说的话" = 政治敏感的社媒批评（担心得罪人 / 超出可用性本职）。
2. 更深的"不该说" = **对可用性行业自己的坏消息**：他在同一期承认自己"只在极少数场合公开说"行业在退步。

### 4.2 5 年间对"要不要出新版"三次改口（明确、可查的自相矛盾→转向）

| 时间 | 原话 | 出处 |
|---|---|---|
| 2020（Brave UX 录制前后） | "As of a couple years ago, my thinking was always, **there's not really that much that I could add at this point.** There's not really that much that I would change or could add that would be useful enough to bother doing it." | [Brave UX Ep.015](https://thespaceinbetween.co.nz/brave-ux-podcast/015-steve-krug) `[一手]` |
| 2020（同期，站点 FAQ） | "**I may do another edition someday, but it's an awful lot of work, so it won't be soon.**" + "it's kind of like deciding to invade Normandy… So I guess the answer is 'We'll see.'" | [sensible.com FAQ](https://sensible.com/all-the-faq-lists-from-my-site/) `[一手]` |
| 2021-03（同一次访谈后段） | "**things started occurring to me that I could do it or ways that I actually could update them** … So now they've gone from, 'I don't see any reason to do that' to, 'yeah, I probably should do that,' but I'd rather do the writing book first." | [Brave UX Ep.015](https://thespaceinbetween.co.nz/brave-ux-podcast/015-steve-krug) `[一手]` |
| 2026（实际结果） | **第四版已定稿，Peachpit / New Riders 2026-11-30 出版**，书名为 *Don't Make Me Think, rev 4: A Common Sense Approach to **UX Research and Design***（副标题从 "Web Usability" 改为 "UX Research and Design"） | [Peachpit 产品页](https://www.peachpit.com/store/dont-make-me-think-rev-4-a-common-sense-approach-to-9780135958643) `[一手]` |

> **注意这条矛盾**：他在 2021 年说"我看不出有什么理由要做"，一年内改口成"我大概应该做"，最终做了 5–6 年。他本人在 2026 年承认："he's been working on the latest edition for **five years**"（Business Insider `[二手]`），而他的**写作之书**他从 2020 年说"五年了"，到 2025 年回读者留言时改口成 **"nine years now"**——[sensible.com 写作之书页评论区](https://sensible.com/the-book-about-writing/) `[一手]`。**他自己对"这本书写了几年"的计数在不同年份相互冲突。**

### 4.3 对"社交媒体批评 vs. 自己的书名"这组矛盾的处理（2026，最值得记的一次自我辩护）

外界（含《纽约时报》）拿书名反刺他：是"Don't Make Me Think"催生了会上瘾的滑动式界面。他的回应是**明确拒绝承认、并当场重划边界**：

> 记者问：如果 Meta 的工程师认为自己是在实践"Don't Make Me Think"来设计 Instagram 无限滚动，他们是不是误解了他？
> **Krug 给了斩钉截铁的"是"。** 他重申书里的立场：如果你是 UX 设计师，做"**user advocate**"是你的第一职责（因此 Meta 工程师**不是** user advocate，因为他们让刷屏的人变得更糟）。
> "**I feel like the book has its own moral tone, and that it attracts people who are more ethical.**"
> —— [Business Insider, 2026-07-29](https://www.businessinsider.com/steve-krug-dont-make-me-think-author-frictionless-internet-2026-7)（经 [b17news 全文转载](https://b17news.com/the-tao-of-steve-krug/) 取得） `[二手]`

**他的核心论点**：他把"frictionless"拆成两个完全不同的定义——① 让该做的事更好做（=可用性，他的本意）；② 让产品好用到人停不下来（=利润最大化）。**"It's not frictionlessness that's ruined the internet, it's bad actors who have made bad products addictively friction-free."**（`[二手]`，同上）

他还给出了**极度反直觉的政策主张**（可作为"他不怕说逆风话"的例证）：理想社媒应该**限制每人最多 100 个关注者**以压制病毒传播，短视频**只能通过搜索和订阅找到、不能靠算法推荐**，"wouldn't be spoon-fed to you in such large doses"。并且：

> "**I think it's pretty admirable**" —— 评价那些主动增加摩擦的 dumbphone / 断网运动。"What they're saying is: 'This stuff is not improving our lives.'"
> —— 同上 `[二手]`

> **一个漂亮的悖论**：写"别让我想"的人，为"主动要求多想一点"的用户鼓掌。他自己并不觉得这是自相矛盾——他区分的是"无意义的思考负担"与"被剥夺的思考"。见 2.2 的划界。

### 4.4 "我这辈子几乎没改过主意"——他对自身立场变化的自我总结（关键一手陈述）

> "So, has Krug changed his mind about that whole not thinking thing? **No, he tells me. He's only ever changed his mind about one thing: that UX would always be side-lined. Then came Steve Jobs and Jony Ive.**"
> "**I don't mind changing my mind. It just hasn't happened very much around this.**"
> —— [Business Insider, 2026-07-29](https://www.businessinsider.com/steve-krug-dont-make-me-think-author-frictionless-internet-2026-7)（经 b17news 转载取得） `[二手]`

**他唯一承认的、真正的立场翻转（而且是他自己反复讲的故事）**：

> "In fact, **I always used to say I didn't think that was ever gonna happen. I didn't think UX and usability was ever gonna become a required line item, top level line item in budgets the way marketing or whatever.** But now to a pretty good extent it has."
> —— [Brave UX Ep.015](https://thespaceinbetween.co.nz/brave-ux-podcast/015-steve-krug) `[一手]`

**矛盾的同一件事**（早期说法 vs. 2026 说法）：
- 2021（Brave UX）：UX 变成预算里的顶格科目，是"Steve Jobs 和 iPhone 证明了 UX 能让人多花钱"，也部分归功于 lean movement。
- 2026（BI）：他把功劳明确归给 **Jobs 与 Jony Ive 两人**，称之为 "the ROI case study"。

> **两者不矛盾，但是重点迁移**：2021 讲的是"行业机制"（lean、预算科目），2026 讲的是"个人英雄"（Jobs + Ive）。`[推断]`

  > "I think a lot about how much **it's gone downhill** since there. **I have done two talks about usability getting better and where we are heading in the right direction. I've only done two because I don't like to say it very much in public. I feel like I will come in for a lot of flak.**"

  另外他也主动披露了工作坊价格（"**It costs about $15k for a full day workshop**"，[sensible.com FAQ](https://sensible.com/all-the-faq-lists-from-my-site/) `[一手]`）和自己的持股（"**I own the usertesting.com website's stock**"，[Marketing Speak Ep.349](https://www.marketingspeak.com/web-usability-essentials-with-steve-krug/) `[一手]`）。

  > "**I have always enjoyed Jakob. I like Jakob but I was indebted to him. … the biggest difference was he was a high end consultant.** And [I]… felt like that was always beyond me. You know, had to be able to go in and sell to business people to be a high end consultant and networking. **I knew this was not in my portfolio.**"
  > —— [Brave UX Ep.015](https://thespaceinbetween.co.nz/brave-ux-podcast/015-steve-krug) `[一手]`
  > 注意：他并不说 Nielsen 的坏话，而是**把自己降下去**。这是他处理"评价同行"这类问题的固定方式。

## 6. 他对自己身份 / 职业的自我叙述

### 6.1 "误打误撞"的官方版本（本人手写，逐条）

来自 [sensible.com/about](https://sensible.com/about/) `[一手]`：

> "My 'career' has definitely not been a straight line.
> - Set out to **replace Don Herbert** of the tv show *Watch Mr. Wizard* (think Bill Nye, only in the 1950s) when he retired.
> - Got an **English Lit degree** in college, after having to quit Physics because **nobody explained calculus to me.** (It's a long story.)
> - Got a job as a **proofreader** from a friend who ran a typesetting shop. (After all, I did have a degree in English.)
> - **Learned about computers while running the typesetting equipment.**
> - Started writing computer manuals when a friend who was a tech writer had more work than she could do herself. (She literally said, 'After all, you have a degree in English.') **Spent ten years as a tech writer.**
> - Etc., etc."

**口语版更精彩**（Brave UX `[一手]`）：

> 关于英语学位："I got an English lit degree in college, **which I proudly can say I've never made any use of it at all or perceived any benefit to me from it in the rest of my life.**"
> 关于如何入行："I realized that **the truth of my career was I never networked. I would've been terrible at it.** … **I'm completely temperamentally [unsuited] to it and would've found it embarrassing.** And I realized that **all the job changes that I got were through nepotism.**"
> 关于他的建议："**develop friends who are smarter and more ambitious than you are and they'll get really good jobs and then they'll be looking around for somebody to hire.**"

**进入 UX 的真正契机**（最关键的职业转折叙述）：

> "my into UX had started actually with my last tech writing job … the developers … realized that **because I was writing the manual, I was spending more time thinking about the interface than they were.** Basically, **if you're writing a user guide for something, you're explaining where the interface doesn't work the way you would expect it to work. That's all you really have to write.** And so they invited me to sit in on the design meetings for the interface."
> —— [Brave UX Ep.015](https://thespaceinbetween.co.nz/brave-ux-podcast/015-steve-krug) `[一手]`

**他自己学可用性测试的方式**——看书自学：

> "I actually just dug [out] and bought **Jakob Nielsen's early book, *Usability Engineering*, where he describes usability testing. And I bought myself one and did some usability tests for them.**"
> —— 同上 `[一手]`

### 6.2 "Mr. Wizard / 现场演示"是他的身份内核（不是比喻，是自我定义）

> "as much as I ever had a career objective, **it was to get his job when he retired**, cuz I just thought that was the best job."
> "I love doing the live demos. … if you haven't watched a usability test before, seeing one is pretty eye-opening. … **it always seemed to me to be the best way to start off.**"
> —— [Brave UX Ep.015](https://thespaceinbetween.co.nz/brave-ux-podcast/015-steve-krug) `[一手]`

注意 Don Herbert **从没退休**（"he actually never retired"），接着是 Bill Nye 接棒——他讲这一段时是在自嘲"连那个梦想岗位都没轮到我"。`[一手]`

### 6.3 关于写作的痛苦（本维度的情绪核心）

一手原话（Brave UX `[一手]`）：

> "Because **it's really hard work.** … **it amounts to thinking and thinking is hard work, thinking clearly is really hard work.** And the other part of it is then thinking clearly and figuring out what you mean. And then coming up with a really good explanation for it, **which is the Mr. Wizard part.** He figured out how to make the principles clear and **that's clarity is really tough.**"
> "over the years, I've always said that I hated writing and **that I can't understand why anybody would write unless they had at least a metaphorical gun … pointed at their head.** Cuz I just find it so painful. **But I had a breakthrough sometime in the last five years when I suddenly realized that I don't actually hate writing that much. What I hate is procrastinating. And for me, 90% of writing is procrastinating, and procrastinating is really painful.**"

**关于截止日期对他毫无作用（罕见地把自己说得很糟）**：

> "for me, deadlines weren't useful at all because I wouldn't kick into gear when the deadline was approaching … **It was only once all of that had been passed and I had completely messed things up, that I would finally lapse into enough panic that I would actually get it done.** So I was really worst case scenario and **I feel very bad. I wanna apologize in the book for all the people who I have made miserable.**"
> —— 同上 `[一手]`

**关于拖延症最反直觉的一条结论**：

> "**you have to get over the idea that you're gonna fix it. You have to get over the idea that you are going to stop procrastinating.** And part of the reason for that is that it's not one thing, [it's] a number of things working at the same time. … **maybe for some people [it's laziness], I'm sure there's some percentage of people who that's true … but that's not it.**"
> —— 同上 `[一手]`

**关于那本"关于写作的书"的执念**（贯穿 2020–2026）：

> "I don't have a due date. When people ask, I just say **I'm hoping to get it done while I still have enough brain cells left to do it.**"
> —— [sensible.com 写作之书页, 2020-10-30](https://sensible.com/the-book-about-writing/) `[一手]`
> "**As I like to tell people, I'd love to read it too.** 😊"（回读者留言）
> —— 同上，2022-02-02 `[一手]`
> "I guess it's **nine years** now that I've been at it in one way or another. Whew."
> —— 同上，2025-01-17 `[一手]`

### 6.4 「我其实是个内向的人」

**关键更正：他没有直接用 "I'm an introvert" 这个词。** 他给的是**行为描述**，比标签更有信息量（Brave UX `[一手]`）：

> "I don't know if I aspired to be a high end consultant, [I] felt like that was always beyond me. You know, [you] had to be able to go in and sell to business people to be a high end consultant and network. **I knew this was not in my portfolio. I would not be good at doing that.** And **it would require completely different kind of confidence than I had. I had a lot of confidence in what I did, but I wouldn't have had confidence doing that. It would've been way too much stress.**"

配合"我从不 networking，我性格上完全不适合，我会觉得难为情"（6.1），这两段合起来就是他的内向自述。
> **信息不足**：未找到他说出 "introvert" 或"社恐"之类自我标签的原话。**不要替他贴标签。**

### 6.5 他对自己影响力的评价（一贯的降级处理）

- "I like to characterize it as **a relatively big fish in a very small pond.**"（Brave UX `[一手]`）
- "**The idea of having 'fans' is something I never envisioned for myself, but it's certainly fun, and I'm very grateful for it.**"（[sensible.com 评论区回帖](https://sensible.com/all-the-faq-lists-from-my-site/)，2020-12-01 `[一手]`）
- 被问"成为 UX 圈家喻户晓的名字感觉如何"，他唯一的回答就是那句"小池塘里比较大的鱼"。**他甚至没有正面回答那个问题。**（Brave UX `[一手]`）
- "**Unless it was actually curing cancer or something, that would be better.**"（自嘲书的成就）——同上 `[一手]`
- 关于写传记用第三人称的理由（难得的坦诚）：
  > "I suppose I settled on third person because **a bio is inherently sort of 'braggy'** ('Steve was born in a log cabin, but grew up to be….'), so it sounds a little better if it seems like someone else is saying it."
  > —— [sensible.com/about](https://sensible.com/about/) `[一手]`

## 7. 他对行业现状的评论

### 7.1 「可用性已经成为 UX 的一个全资子公司」——**原话已核实**

这是本研究里最需要精确的一句。**原始出处是《Don't Make Me Think》第 4 版的官方描述**，措辞是 Krug 本人的说法：

> "…given all the changes between then and now (**usability had become, as he puts it, 'a wholly owned subsidiary of User Experience Design'**, a million new UXers were minted during the UX hiring gold rush, and AI suddenly appeared, promising to either make all of them incredibly productive, put them all out of work, or wipe them out with the rest of mankind)…"
> —— [Peachpit / New Riders 官方产品描述](https://www.peachpit.com/store/dont-make-me-think-rev-4-a-common-sense-approach-to-9780135958643) `[一手]`（出版社引述 Krug 本人措辞）

**英文原话（建议引用这个版本）**：
> **"a wholly owned subsidiary of User Experience Design"**

**同一现象在他 2021 年的口语里是这样说的**（不同措辞，同一判断）：

> Brendan："Have we seen the end of the usability consultant?"
> Krug："**Well no. I mean they're called UX researchers now. They're called either they're UX researchers and UX designers. And it's hard to say what that really, really means.**"
> —— [Brave UX Ep.015](https://thespaceinbetween.co.nz/brave-ux-podcast/015-steve-krug) `[一手]`

**"design"这个词被稀释**（他 2021 年的抱怨，很尖锐）：

> "**design has become such a freaking sloppy term these days. It's like, is everybody a designer? Is nobody a designer? I think those are the two options.**"
> 主持人接："You can thank 3M for that."（可以谢 3M 的便利贴）
> Krug："**all you need [is] a wall and some post-it notes.**"
> —— 同上 `[一手]`

### 7.2 关于"行业在退步"（他自己不愿公开讲的判断）

> "**I think a lot these days about how much it's gone downhill since then. … I've only done two [talks about it] because I don't like to say it very much in public. I feel like I will come in for a lot of flak.**"
> —— [Marketing Speak Ep.349](https://www.marketingspeak.com/web-usability-essentials-with-steve-krug/) `[一手]`

2026 年的总结更直白：

> 问：互联网有没有比他写书时更好用？
> **"It's gotten worse, and I think it will get even worse with AI."**
> 理由：很多网站表面被磨平、简化了，但"真正要做的事"——联系客服、提交在线表单、取消订阅——依旧遥不可及。
> —— [Business Insider, 2026-07-29](https://www.businessinsider.com/steve-krug-dont-make-me-think-author-frictionless-internet-2026-7)（经 b17news 转载） `[二手]`

### 7.3 「UX 是不是变坏了 / 伦理问题」

> "**I think people are under pressure to use user research for purposes that are not in the user's interest.** How do we shave another fraction of a percent of clicks off of this? Whether it's good, whether it's in the user's interest or not. … **it's one of the unintended consequences when people say, 'oh well UX, okay UX stuff works.' Then all of a sudden the people who have those impure motivations come out of the woodwork and they start leaning on the people doing the UX to do that.**"
> 结果是："**there are certainly dark practices [and they] are a real thing and it's unfortunate and so the backlash to that is a big surge in discussion of ethics in the UX field.**"
> —— [Brave UX Ep.015](https://thespaceinbetween.co.nz/brave-ux-podcast/015-steve-krug) `[一手]`

**他给出的立场解**（被追问 UX researcher 在团队里该站哪）：

> "**you need to picture yourself as you're serving the process, you're serving the need. As the UX researcher, you're serving the needs of the designers and the developers.** … **The one thing you don't want to do is get polarized and feel like you're telling them what to do.** … I think the way you assert that you have value is **by supporting these other people and by making it clear that what you're there to do is to provide them with insights that make their work better.**"
> —— 同上 `[一手]`

### 7.4 关于 UX 的规模膨胀

> "I don't know how many people are doing UX work now, but **I'm sure it's five to 10 times as many as there were 20 years ago, probably 10 times as many.**"
> "**I'm still surprised at how much stuff is not what it should be.** … It's not that hard to run into a glaring usability problem. But I guess it's cuz people are building so much stuff. **They're building far more stuff than they were 20 years ago.**"
> —— [Brave UX Ep.015](https://thespaceinbetween.co.nz/brave-ux-podcast/015-steve-krug) `[一手]`

第 4 版官方描述把同一件事说得更狠：**"a million new UXers were minted during the UX hiring gold rush"**（[Peachpit](https://www.peachpit.com/store/dont-make-me-think-rev-4-a-common-sense-approach-to-9780135958643) `[一手]`）。

### 7.5 关于"可用性测试成为常识"这件事的进展

> "I think that's become much more accepted. Certainly **it was relatively rare for that to be part of the development process 20 years ago.** … **one of the reasons why I did *Rocket Surgery* is cuz it was pretty much always handed off to consultants. So it was expensive. So you might do one round of usability testing during the development life cycle of a product.**"
> —— 同上 `[一手]`

### 7.6 关于"要不要改进世界"——他把这行看得很朴素

> "**how often do you get a chance in your job to dramatically improve other people's lives by just doing your work a little better?**"（谈无障碍）
> 他也把这个姿态用在别处："**Unless it was actually curing cancer or something, that would be better.**"
> —— [Built In, 2020-04-07](https://builtin.com/design-ux/simplicity-ux-steve-krug-interview) / [Brave UX Ep.015](https://thespaceinbetween.co.nz/brave-ux-podcast/015-steve-krug) `[一手]`

**他对无障碍的立场也很"他"**：他**不主张**用吓人的统计数字（"They say, like, you know, 60 percent of people have some disability. **That's sort of a strange count.**"），主张直接用道德理由：

> "**You should do this because it's the right thing. It improves people's lives.**"
> —— 同上 `[一手]`

## 8. 保留的矛盾清单（不做调和）

1. **"Don't Make Me Think" 是元凶还是被误用？** 外界（含 NYT）拿书名讽刺它催生了上瘾式设计；Krug 坚称是"坏演员把产品做得成瘾性地无摩擦"，并称自己是 user advocate。他**不接受任何共谋指控**，同时**承认社媒是他见过最坏的东西**。两句放在一起张力很大。
2. **"不常上播客" vs. 实际节目数量。** 官网说 "I don't do many podcasts"，但本研究已确认存在 10 个以上节目 + 多个演讲。`[推断]` 他的"不多"是和营销型作者比。
3. **"写这本书写了几年"在他自己的口径里从 5 年 → 6 年 → 9 年**（且这可能与"Don't Make Me Think 第 4 版"的 5 年混在一起）。见 4.2。
4. **AI：2026-03 "power steering / 不取代人的判断" vs. 2026-07 "有 AI 会更糟"。** 见 4.5。
5. **UX 的功劳归因：机制（lean / 预算）vs. 个人（Jobs & Ive）。** 见 4.4。
6. **"没什么可加的了" → "我大概该做新版"（一年内翻转）。** 见 4.2。
7. **他对"frictionless"的批判与自己书名的一致性**：他自己认为完全一致（区分"无意义的思考"与"该有的思考"），但这依赖一个**只有读过书、而非只看标题**才能获得的区分。他自己也承认标题"worked out very well"，即承认标题的简化有传播价值。`[推断]`

## 10. 给下游蒸馏的一句话总结

**Krug 的口语模式可以概括为：先自贬 → 再拆掉对方的框架（而不是反驳对方的结论）→ 用一个小到能立刻执行的动作收尾 → 最后加一句电影或电视的类比。**

三个最可复用的行为特征：
1. **"Against interest" 是主动选择，不是失言。** 他把"说对自己不利的话"当成人格标签和播客原则，甚至要求"头上挂个计数器"。→ 蒸馏时不要把他的自贬当成谦虚修辞，那是**可信度策略**。
2. **被质疑时从不辩护结论，而是替换评价标准。** "3 个用户不显著" → "这里根本没有统计"；"书名太简单" → "区别在于该想什么、不该想什么"；"更新书很麻烦" → "像入侵诺曼底，所以先别宣布"。→ 这是**换尺子**，不是**改答案**。
3. **他的诚实是有边界的诚实。** 他会说"我不擅长这个""我编的""我进不来这行""我性格不适合"，但**不评同行、不评自己没做过的管理问题、不给入行建议**；对具体客户名和金钱则相当公开。→ 边界不在"隐私"，在**"我有没有资格说"**。
